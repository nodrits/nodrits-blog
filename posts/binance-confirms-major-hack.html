Binance Confirms Major Hack as Over $2.5 Billion Is Frozen

October 30, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Hours after calling it a “potential security issue,” the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange has admitted what everyone suspected: Binance said on Thursday, October 29, 2026, that it had fallen victim to a major hack. More than $2.5 billion has been frozen in an effort to stop the stolen funds from moving further, and Binance has suspended trading on certain products while it traces the attack with law enforcement, stablecoin issuers and blockchain analytics firms.

What happened

The confirmation marks a sharp change in tone from Binance’s first statement, which said only that its teams were urgently reviewing a potential security issue and that user funds were safe. Binance now says it was the victim of a major hack, and that assets frozen in connection with the attack total more than $2.5 billion — a figure that could still rise. Tether, issuer of the USDT stablecoin, said it froze about $1.6 billion related to the incident, while Circle froze more than $800 million in USDC. Crypto firm Ripple froze roughly $200 million, according to Binance co-founder Yi He. Binance has also suspended trading on certain products, including Binance Alpha trading, until the security issue is resolved. Binance has still not said how much was actually stolen, and some on-chain estimates run far higher than the frozen total — but those figures remain unconfirmed. Binance.US, a separate exchange, says it is not affected.

Why it matters

Some observers say this could become the biggest crypto theft on record, exceeding the roughly $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in February 2025. Bitcoin fell more than 3% on the news. The scale of the freeze also shows how the industry’s emergency response now works: stablecoin issuers can freeze funds at the token level within hours of an attack being flagged. Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao, who no longer runs the company but remains its majority shareholder, wrote on X that the incident “does look like a hack” and that he expects user funds to be fully covered. Binance itself insists: “User funds are completely safe,” and its co-CEO Yi He said Binance will cover all losses from the exploit.

What it means for you

If you trade or hold crypto on any exchange, treat this as a reminder to check the basics now: enable two-factor authentication, confirm withdrawal addresses before sending funds, and avoid signing unexpected wallet prompts. Keep large balances off exchanges where possible, and follow official updates from Binance, Tether and Circle rather than social-media estimates of the stolen total — the confirmed facts so far are the hack itself, the frozen funds and the trading suspensions, not a final theft figure.
