DeepSeek Just Raised $15 Billion to Take On America's AI Labs

October 22, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The lab is DeepSeek. According to Bloomberg, the Hangzhou-based Chinese AI pioneer has raised about 100 billion yuan — roughly $15 billion — in its latest funding round, one of the largest in China's history, with battery giant CATL and WeChat operator Tencent among the biggest investors. And it did so just two days after the US ordered federal agencies to remove DeepSeek's technology from their systems, as TechCrunch notes.

What happened

Bloomberg reports that DeepSeek raised its financing target at least twice while talking to investors — from 50 billion yuan to 80 billion yuan, before landing at about 100 billion yuan (roughly $15 billion). People familiar with the matter told Bloomberg that Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd. (CATL) and Tencent Holdings were among the biggest backers in the round; the details are not public, and representatives of DeepSeek, Tencent and CATL did not respond to requests for comment. TechCrunch adds that the raise lands just two days after the US ordered federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek's AI technology over security concerns — and that the company is ramping up spending on AI infrastructure and open models for markets at home and abroad.

Why it matters

DeepSeek is the lab that, in 2025, triggered a roughly $1 trillion meltdown in global markets by showing it could rival Silicon Valley's best models at a fraction of the cost — all without anything close to the US labs' financial firepower. Bloomberg points out that Anthropic and OpenAI have each raised more than $100 billion; DeepSeek never had that kind of war chest. A $15 billion round doesn't close that gap, but it changes the maths: more data centers, more compute, and more of the low-cost open models that have been winning users in the US and other overseas markets — and pressuring rivals at home, where its September V4.1 Flash release set off a drop in local competitors' share prices. Bloomberg News has also reported that DeepSeek, like rival Moonshot, is preparing for an IPO that would be a milestone for Chinese markets.

What it means for you

If you build with AI, this is the open-model pressure valve getting stronger. A well-funded DeepSeek means continued downward pressure on AI prices and more capable open models that businesses can run and customize — good news for startups and agencies that build on top of them, and a harder fight for every closed, premium-priced API. If you follow the US–China tech race, note the sequence: Washington moves to shut DeepSeek out of federal systems, and within days Chinese corporate giants write it one of the largest cheques in the country's startup history.

The Nodrits take

Money follows capability, and DeepSeek proved capability first — cheaply. The next phase of the AI race won't be won on model quality alone, but on who can afford the compute to serve the world. Will $15 billion be enough to shake Silicon Valley's lead? Tell us in the comments.
