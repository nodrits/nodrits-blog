General Compute Raises $100M Series A to Build an AI Cloud Without NVIDIA Chips

October 27, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Everyone wrote neoclouds off this week — and then General Compute raised $100 million anyway. The AI infrastructure startup closed a Series A led by Blackbird Ventures and Abstract Ventures at an $800 million post-money valuation, up from just $60 million after its seed round in May 2026. The bet behind the money is a contrarian one: an AI cloud built not on NVIDIA graphics processors, but on specialized inference chips from NVIDIA’s rivals.

What happened

General Compute announced the $100 million Series A on October 27, 2026, TechCrunch reported. The round follows a deal with a large customer worth $100 million in annual recurring revenue, which paved the way for the raise. The company supplies bare-metal servers — hardware without software — to cloud companies that need high-speed AI inference performance, and it builds that cloud out of specialized inference chips from SambaNova, Positron and Cerebras. Its $400 million purchase agreement with SambaNova was, per TechCrunch, the first debt financing of inference chips.

Why it matters

The timing is what makes this raise stand out. CoreWeave, the publicly traded neocloud, has fallen to a 12-month low, and Elon Musk is flexing roughly $40 billion in debt for computing capacity while predicting that “200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation.” General Compute co-founder and CEO Finn Puklowski is unfazed: he calls fears of a chip-led collapse “massively overstated” and says customers call him every week asking for TPUs, GPUs and any chip that gets them more compute. He also credits Anthropic’s bullish S-1 filing with “settling fears for today.”

What it means for you

Instead of building huge data centers from scratch, General Compute rents space inside other operators’ facilities, taps smaller power sources, and converts former bitcoin-mining sites into places that run AI models. Puklowski expects the company to deploy 75 megawatts of computing capacity in 2027 and says it will have access to 200 megawatts of power — more, he admits, than it can probably fund and equip. For businesses buying AI capacity, the message is that inference — running finished models fast and cheaply — is becoming its own market, separate from the training-chip arms race.

The Nodrits take

Our take: this is a bet that the AI infrastructure market is big enough for a second lane. If inference demand keeps growing the way customers’ calls suggest, a cheaper, non-NVIDIA cloud has a real opening — but it still has to prove it can scale power and hardware as fast as its ambitions.
