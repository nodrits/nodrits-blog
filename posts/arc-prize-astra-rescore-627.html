That 99.9% AI Score Was Rechecked. The Real Number Is 62.7%.

October 26, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 5 min read

OpenAI claimed its GPT-6 Astra scored 99.9 percent on ARC-AGI-3 when it launched in September. The ARC Prize Foundation, which built the test, has now rerun the model on its standard, provider-neutral harness — and scored it 62.7 percent. Same model, different scaffolding, 37 points apart.

What happened

ARC Prize ran Astra twice and published both results. On its standard harness, Astra scored 62.7 percent at maximum reasoning effort (54.8 percent at high effort). On OpenAI’s provider adapter harness, the same model scored 98.6 percent at maximum effort and 99.9 percent at high effort. The difference is what the model is allowed to carry between requests: under the standard harness each request starts cold and the model keeps only the notes it chooses to preserve, while the provider adapter preserves an opaque reasoning state between requests and compacts long conversations.

ARC Prize’s verdict is blunt: the 99.9 percent figure is not a comparable score, because it was not produced under the conditions every other lab’s model must clear. The adapter result is not worthless — on the 167 pairs both harnesses solved, adapter runs were about 3.66 times faster and used 49 percent fewer tokens — but a model-plus-scaffolding result presented as a model result is a different claim. Note too that 62.7 percent is still the highest score on ARC-AGI-3 by a wide margin, and ARC Prize itself calls it a genuine step forward.

Why it matters

A benchmark score is not a property of a model the way height is a property of a person. It measures a system: the model plus the harness plus the budget. ARC-AGI-3 was built specifically to resist memorization and reward novel reasoning, placing an agent inside unfamiliar turn-based environments where it must explore, infer rules, plan, act and adjust. When the harness changes the score by 37 points, every headline number in the AI race deserves a second question — and investors are pricing capability off these charts.

What it means for you

Before trusting any benchmark, ask how it was run. Compare scores only on identical harnesses, pilot models on your own tasks before buying, and treat any 99 percent claim as a question, not an answer. The harness is part of the product you are buying.

The Nodrits take

The scandal here is not the score — 62.7 percent is still a milestone. It is presenting a system result as a model result. Can benchmark scores be trusted when the harness changes the result? Tell us your take in the comments.
