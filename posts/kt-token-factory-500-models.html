KT's Token Factory Launches Today: One Bill, One API, and 500 AI Models to Choose From

October 30, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Every AI model you use, now on one bill. KT, South Korea's telecom giant, launches its Token Factory platform for corporate customers on October 30, 2026 — a single place to access hundreds of AI models, track what they cost, and pay for them together, instead of juggling a separate subscription for each one.

What happened

KT introduced Token Factory in Seoul on October 27, 2026, and scheduled its corporate launch for October 30. The platform brings access to domestic and international AI models — including ChatGPT, per MoneyToday — behind a single application programming interface, with usage monitoring, billing and security controls in one place. Reporting differs on the launch catalogue: Telecompaper and MoneyToday describe access to more than 500 models, while Superpower Daily reports the service will start with 73 models and expand from there. The honest reading is that 500+ is the platform's catalogue ambition and 73 is the launch count — the gap matters less than the mechanism. At its core is KT's in-house Auto Model Router, which analyzes a query's type, difficulty, required quality and cost, then automatically selects the appropriate model: low-cost models for simple tasks, high-performance models for complex analysis. KT also says input-data compression can reduce token usage by up to 25%. Customers can check usage across models and pay in Korean won through unified billing, with plans to expand beyond corporate clients later.

Why it matters

Teams no longer use one model for everything. Coding, translation, document summarization and complex reasoning each suit a different model, so companies end up with a patchwork of subscriptions, contracts and bills — and no single view of what AI actually costs. That is the same multi-model sprawl behind this week's other stories: GitHub's Copilot Fusion routes coding tasks across models, and Anthropic's enterprise reports try to show what token spend buys. KT's bet is that the gateway, not the model, is where enterprises need help: one API, one router, one bill. The risk is equally clear — a gateway is a middleman, and it only earns its place if the router's savings beat its own cost and complexity.

What it means for you

Before you add another model subscription, audit the ones you already pay for. List every model your team uses, what each one is actually used for, and what it costs per task — then route routine work to cheaper models and reserve frontier models for the hard cases. Set one owner for AI spend before it sprawls, and judge any gateway by a single test: does it lower your cost per completed task, or just move the bill?

The Nodrits take

One bill or one more vendor? A single gateway saves admin, but it adds a dependency between your team and every model you use. The test is simple: does the router save more than it costs?
