Billions Frozen After Security Scare Hits the World’s Biggest Crypto Exchange

October 30, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange is investigating a potential security issue — and the fallout is already measured in billions. Stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze more than $2.38 billion after Binance said on Thursday, October 29, 2026, that its teams were urgently reviewing a potential security issue. Binance says user funds are safe, while its co-founder Changpeng Zhao wrote on X that the incident “looked like a hack.”

What happened

Binance, which says it has more than 335 million registered users worldwide, said in a post on X that its teams were reviewing all relevant information with urgency. Tether, issuer of the world’s largest stablecoin, said it froze nearly $1.57 billion related to the hack, while Circle froze more than $800 million. Crypto firm Ripple froze roughly $200 million, according to Binance co-founder Yi He on X. Circle co-founder and CEO Jeremy Allaire wrote on X that Circle is closely tracking reports of a potential exploit involving Binance and will continue supporting the investigation. Binance.US, a separate exchange, said it was not affected by the security incident.

Why it matters

The speed of the freeze shows how the crypto industry’s emergency playbook now works: when a major exchange flags a possible exploit, stablecoin issuers can freeze funds at the token level before they move further. That is a powerful safety net — and a reminder of how centralized the supposedly decentralized system can be in a crisis. Binance itself has so far called the event a “potential security issue” under investigation, not a confirmed hack, so the exact nature and scale of any theft remain unconfirmed.

What it means for you

If you hold crypto on any exchange, this is a good moment to check the basics: enable two-factor authentication, confirm withdrawal addresses, and consider whether large balances belong on an exchange at all. Watch for official updates from Binance, Tether and Circle rather than rumors on social media — early reports of a “$3 billion hack” circulated before any tier-1 outlet confirmed the incident, and the confirmed facts so far concern frozen funds, not a confirmed stolen total.
