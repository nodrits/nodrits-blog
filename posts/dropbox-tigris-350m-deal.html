Dropbox Buys Tigris Data for $350M in Its Largest Acquisition Ever

November 5, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Dropbox just made the biggest move in its history. The file-storage giant is acquiring cloud technology company Tigris Data for $350 million — what appears to be the largest acquisition Dropbox has ever made — and the deal is not about storing your files. It is about building the storage layer for AI agents.

What happened

Dropbox announced the deal on Thursday, November 5, 2026. The $350 million acquisition is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, and Dropbox says it has no plans to reduce headcount as part of the acquisition. The company already cut 9% of its global workforce last month, according to the Wall Street Journal.

The deal is meant to strengthen Dropbox's AI infrastructure and support the development of DBX Agent Cloud, a new storage platform aimed at businesses and developers building AI models and agents. DBX Agent Cloud will initially offer object storage as a standalone product, letting businesses buy storage independently and use it with existing tools built for Amazon S3.

Tigris Data was founded by former Uber engineers who helped develop the ride-hailing company's storage infrastructure. Its platform automatically positions data closer to the users and computing resources that need it, cutting access times and improving performance. The company raised $25 million in a Series A funding round last year.

Why it matters

Dropbox is betting that the next era of cloud storage is not built for people opening folders — it is built for AI agents that need to read, write and manage files on their own. Dropbox says it plans to expand DBX Agent Cloud with support for documents, images, audio and video, plus filesystem features that let AI agents manage files and keep continuity across tasks and sessions.

The core Dropbox product is getting the same treatment: users will be able to ask AI to perform supported tasks using their files, choose between different AI models, and connect their Dropbox content to third-party tools including ChatGPT, Claude and Muse. Dropbox says the updated experience has already reached more than 70,000 teams, with the initial rollout available to eligible Standard and Advanced customers in the US, UK and Australia, and broader availability planned for 2027.

This is not Dropbox's first AI acquisition — it previously bought Reclaim.ai, Promoted.ai and Mobius Labs — but at $350 million it is by far the biggest, and it signals that Dropbox wants to be infrastructure for the agent era, not just a folder in the cloud.

What it means for you

If your business stores files in Dropbox, the practical change is that your storage is being rebuilt around AI agents: files that an agent can search, act on and carry across sessions, with your choice of AI model. For developers, DBX Agent Cloud's S3-compatible object storage is a new option for agent workloads that need data close to compute. And if you run a SaaS product, the lesson is strategic — the storage layer is becoming an AI battleground, and even a decades-old file company is spending its biggest cheque ever to stay in it.

The Nodrits take

Dropbox spent $350 million because the file is no longer the product — the agent that works on the file is.
