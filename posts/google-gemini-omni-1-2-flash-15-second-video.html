Google's AI Video Just Got Longer — and 30% Cheaper

November 7, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Google's AI video just got longer — and 30% cheaper. The company released Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash on November 5, 2026, generally available across the Gemini API and Gemini for business. Clips now run up to 15 seconds in a single generation, up from 10 — and the price fell to $0.07 per second at 720p, about $1.05 for a full clip.

What happened

Google announced Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash on November 5, 2026, on its developer blog, as covered by multiple outlets the same day. The model generates and edits video with native audio: text-to-video, image-to-video and video editing, in 16:9 and 9:16, at 360p, 720p, 1080p or 4K, at 24 frames per second. The headline changes: native clips of 3 to 15 seconds, in one-second steps, up from a 10-second ceiling; multi-turn conversational editing, so you can revise the same scene instead of restarting from a blank prompt; stronger character consistency across up to 6 reference images; first-and-last-frame interpolation of 3 to 10 seconds; and extension of 3 to 10 seconds per turn, up to 30 seconds in total. Pricing fell 30% from Omni 1.1: video output is $12 per million tokens, down from $17.50, which Google counts as about $0.07 per second at 720p. The model is in Google Vids — 15-second clips at no additional cost with a Google account or Workspace subscription — and in Google Flow, where subscribers get the longer duration on their credit schedules. From December 3, 2026, requests to the old model ID, gemini-omni-1.1-flash, will automatically route to 1.2 and bill at 1.2 rates.

Why it matters

Fifteen seconds is the length that matters. Ten seconds forced a cut or a stitch; fifteen is enough for a complete product explainer, a full social ad spot, or a scene with a beginning, a middle and an end — in one generation. But the longer clip is the less interesting half of the release. The editing is the tool: multi-turn conversational editing means a creator can fix a shot instead of re-rolling it, and that changes the economics more than the price cut does, because every re-roll bills. Google published no benchmarks for the quality claims, so treat them as a reason to test, not as results — and the help pages carry their own limits: clips are 3 to 10 seconds for some users, with up to 15 seconds for AI Ultra subscribers, and video editing and avatar creation are unavailable in the EEA, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and some US states. The model is listed on the paid tier of the Gemini API, with no free tier.

What it means for you

If you make social content, 15 seconds changes the unit of work: one prompt, one finished ad spot, instead of a clip you must cut and stitch. Budget per attempt, not per success — the meter runs on drafts, not finals, and every miss still bills, so a $1.05 clip is cheap only if it lands on the first or second try. Test character consistency on your own brand shots before promising a client a look: up to 6 reference images is the tool for that, and it is the feature to evaluate first. If you use Google Vids, the longer clips arrive at no additional cost — check your account, since Google is moving accounts to Omni 1.2 over a few days from November 5. And if you still call the 1.1 model ID, plan the migration: from December 3 it routes to 1.2 automatically, at 1.2 rates.

The Nodrits take

Google is not selling better video. It is selling cheaper seconds and longer takes — and for creators, the price cut is the headline while the editing is the tool. The gap that matters is not 10 seconds against 15. It is edit-in-place against start-over, and that is a workflow change, not a spec change. AI video is becoming a utility priced by the second, and the creators who win will be the ones who treat the second as a budget, not a toy. Is 15 seconds enough for a full AI ad?
