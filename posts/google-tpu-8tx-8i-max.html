Google Adds Two New TPU Systems — TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max — Four AI Chips in One Year

November 9, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Google has expanded its eighth-generation TPU portfolio with two new systems — TPU 8tx, built for secure and confidential AI inference, and TPU 8i Max, built for massive context windows and the highest compute density per rack. It is the first time Google has introduced four TPU systems in a single year, and the move sharpens its challenge to Nvidia's dominance of AI hardware. Bloomberg News reported the launch on Monday, and Alphabet shares rose more than 2% in premarket trading.

What happened

Google announced the two systems on November 9, 2026, in an official blog post, adding to the TPU 8t for large-scale training and TPU 8i for real-time reasoning that it introduced at Google Cloud Next in April. TPU 8tx is optimized for regulated enterprises and governments running frontier or proprietary models and autonomous agents alongside their most sensitive data. Built on the same silicon as TPU 8t and paired with 5th Generation AMD EPYC CPU hosts, it builds security directly into the hardware layer, keeping sensitive data and model weights protected and isolated even while in active use — data normally has to be unlocked in memory while a computer processes it. Google says TPU 8tx delivers up to 2x lower latency than TPU 8t with a near-zero performance penalty, and lets teams fine-tune models on private data, distill large models into smaller ones, and train agents through reinforcement learning without moving sensitive data between separate clusters.

TPU 8i Max tackles a different bottleneck: the memory wall. Google combined two low-latency TPU 8i chips onto a single package, delivering double the compute, double the SRAM and double the HBM bandwidth of TPU 8i. Because it fits twice the silicon within the existing rack footprint, data centers can deploy significantly more computing power per square foot and per watt — aimed at workloads where dozens of AI agents work together across an entire software codebase or hours of video at once.

Google also used the announcement to open TPUs up further to developers: it open-sourced TorchTPU so PyTorch code and Hugging Face models run natively on TPUs with no code rewrites, put a TPU agent suite into preview that plugs into coding tools like Claude Code and Cursor, and open-sourced TPU Sync, the model-state layer for TPU clusters. Google says its TPU business now serves nearly 100 customers, including frontier labs, trading firms, retailers and universities.

Why it matters

Google's homegrown chips, once reserved for internal use, have emerged as the most credible challenger to Nvidia's pricey graphics processors that dominate AI workloads worldwide. The April launch already split the eighth generation into two specialized chips — 8t for training, with up to 2.7 times better training performance than the previous generation, and 8i for inference, with 80% better inference price performance. Now Google is showing it can spin new system configurations out of the same silicon in months rather than years: as its blog puts it, it designs the computer around the chip, adjusting packaging, memory, networking and security to meet emerging needs. Four systems in one year is the proof point.

"We've expanded our eighth generation TPU portfolio with TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max, marking four new TPU systems in a single year." — Google, November 9, 2026

The two new systems also map neatly onto where AI work is heading: agents that reason across million-token contexts, and regulated customers who want frontier models running next to data that can never leave their control. Both are problems Nvidia's general-purpose GPUs were not specifically designed to solve.

What it means for you

If you build or buy AI infrastructure, the choice is widening fast. Teams running AI on Google Cloud now have a purpose-built option for confidential workloads and one for long-context agent work, alongside the training and reasoning systems from April — and PyTorch code now runs natively on TPUs without rewrites, which lowers the switching cost of trying them. If you are a business rather than a chip buyer, the practical signal is simpler: the hardware race is pushing inference costs down and making long-context agents cheaper to run, so agent features that were too slow or too expensive six months ago are worth re-pricing now. Watch for Google Cloud pricing and availability details for the two new systems, which Google has not yet spelled out.
