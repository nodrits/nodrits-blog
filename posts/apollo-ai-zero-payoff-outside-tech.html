AI Paid Off for Big Tech. For Everyone Else, the Payoff Is Zero.

October 26, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Despite massive investment and widespread hype, artificial intelligence has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside the tech industry. That is the verdict from Apollo Global Management chief economist Torsten Slok, in a note written October 24 and reported by Bloomberg and TechCrunch on October 25.

What happened

Slok focuses on what he calls the S&P 493 — the S&P 500 minus Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla. In that group, he finds profit margins and revenue per worker appearing flat, with no evidence that AI is improving productivity. "Despite massive investments and widespread hype around artificial intelligence, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech," he wrote. Revenue per worker is rising for the Mag 7 but flat for the S&P 493, and mainstream companies have begun "pulling back and starting to view token spending not as a source of growth but as a cost center that weighs on margins."

The context sharpens the point: Meta spent $34.9 billion on capital expenditures in its third quarter while reporting negative free cash flow of $2.1 billion, and it expects to be even more aggressive on AI capex. Recent studies have similarly found that AI has yet to deliver significant productivity gains across the broader economy.

Why it matters

Slok’s argument cuts to the difference between intelligence and judgment: "AI may be a great calculator, but in business, intelligence is mostly judgment." Business runs on accountability, trust, culture, unwritten rules, ethical trade-offs and fairness — all of which shape reputational risk and ultimately drive revenue and margins, and none of which AI understands. It is also an aggregate claim: flat index-level measures can hide real gains, costs, delays and losses inside individual deployments. The honest reading is not that no company benefits, but that the broad payoff has not shown up in the numbers yet.

What it means for you

Measure the job, not the demo. Set a baseline before any AI project — staff time saved, rework, error rates, customer outcomes, operating cost, risk — and kill pilots that cannot show a real return. Fund the boring workflow wins first, and treat an adoption count as what it is: usage, not ROI.
