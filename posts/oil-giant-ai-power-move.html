Saudi Aramco Takes a 49% Stake in HUMAIN With a $2.6 Billion AI Investment

November 1, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The oil giant is Saudi Aramco, and the bet is HUMAIN, Saudi Arabia’s state-backed AI company. Aramco has acquired a 49% stake in HUMAIN through a wholly owned subsidiary, investing almost 9.9 billion Saudi riyals — about $2.6 billion — in cash this year, plus AI, computing and digital assets, the companies said in a joint statement on Sunday, November 1, 2026. Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF) retains the remaining 51%.

What happened

Aramco announced the completed transaction on Sunday, November 1, 2026. The stake was taken through a wholly owned subsidiary, with PIF keeping majority ownership of HUMAIN. Alongside the cash, Aramco is contributing AI assets, computing capabilities and talent, and the deal will let HUMAIN monetise Aramco’s existing AI and digital assets. Aramco has been planning a significant minority stake in HUMAIN for at least a year; the two sides signed a non-binding term sheet in October 2025. HUMAIN itself launched in May 2025 and is building across data centres, high-performance computing infrastructure and cloud platforms, advanced AI models, and AI solutions for industry — including an Arabic-language large language model.

Why it matters

The deal is one of the largest single AI-infrastructure bets in the Gulf this year, and it ties the world’s biggest oil exporter directly to the AI compute race. HUMAIN is targeting 3 gigawatts of AI data centre capacity by 2030, and Aramco says it will use HUMAIN’s infrastructure to turn advanced AI into industrial applications across its own operations. Aramco chief executive Amin Nasser has previously put the technology-related value AI has generated for the company since 2023 at $11.3 billion. The move also fits a wider regional pattern: Gulf states are racing to build sovereign AI capacity — data centres, models and cloud platforms — rather than renting it from abroad.

What it means for you

For businesses, the signal is practical: AI infrastructure is becoming a national-level investment, and the companies that own compute, data centres and industry-specific models will set the terms for everyone else. If you run a business in the Gulf or serve clients there, expect more locally hosted AI services, Arabic-first AI tools, and industrial AI offerings built on this kind of infrastructure — and plan your own AI adoption around platforms that can run where your data and customers are.
