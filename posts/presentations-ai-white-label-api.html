Slides Your Customers Never Knew Were AI: Presentations.AI Launches a White-Label API

November 2, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Slides your customers never knew were AI. Presentations.AI announced on November 1, 2026 a white-label AI presentation generator and an editable PowerPoint API — so a software platform, an agency or an AI agent can now build branded decks inside its own product, with no trace of the tool that made them.

What happened

The launch, announced from San Francisco via GlobeNewswire, has two parts: a white-label presentation generator and a presentation generation API. Platforms can create branded, editable PowerPoint and Google Slides presentations inside their own products, with OAuth 2.0, API key management, webhooks, audit logs, multi-tenant workspaces and role-based access at the workspace and deck level. Two features do the brand work: Brand Sync, which keeps generated decks in the customer's visual identity, and Presentation Refresh, which updates decks automatically. API pricing starts at $0.10 per generated slide — 10 credits at $0.01 each — with a 50% launch discount for three months, cutting the effective rate to $0.05 per slide, and monthly API packages starting at $49. White-label output is available at the Growth tier and above. Presentations.AI is backed by Accel and Together Fund.

Why it matters

The telling detail is the output format: native, editable PowerPoint, not rendered images. A deck that cannot be edited after generation is a dead end; a deck that opens in PowerPoint and stays revisable slots into how businesses already work. That is why this launch matters beyond slides. AI is moving from standalone apps that users visit into capabilities that other products embed — and the companies that win that shift are the ones whose output survives contact with the customer's real workflow. For agencies, the pitch is sharper still: sell presentation work under your own brand, with the AI invisible.

What it means for you

If you sell reports, proposals or client decks, the move to test is embedding before rebuilding: trial the API on one real client template, and check three things before you commit — that the output is genuinely editable in PowerPoint, that Brand Sync holds your fonts, colours and templates under pressure, and that volume pricing beats per-seat pricing at your slide counts. Keep a human review step for anything client-facing; an invisible tool still needs a visible standard.
