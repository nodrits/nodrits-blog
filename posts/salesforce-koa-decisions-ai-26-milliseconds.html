Salesforce Built an AI That Decides in 26 Milliseconds

November 7, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Salesforce built an AI that doesn't think. It just decides. The company introduced Koa Decisions on November 6, 2026 — a model that reads a request, a list of categories you define, and returns a choice, a yes-or-no, or a score in a single step, in milliseconds. The headline number: about 26 milliseconds to route a request in Agentforce, against about 1.4 seconds for a general-purpose large language model.

What happened

Salesforce announced Koa Decisions on November 6, 2026, in a post on its own news site, as covered by Unite.AI the same day. It is the next version of HyperClassifier, the company's earlier decision model, rebuilt by applying novel training techniques to Google's Gemma, an open-weight model. Where a reasoning model thinks step by step, Koa Decisions decides in one step: it reads a request — text, image or audio — and a user-defined list of categories, then returns a selection, a yes-or-no decision, or a score. Salesforce's data science team reports 85.5% accuracy on a production intent-routing benchmark, with responses in about half a second, and a median speed as much as about 7.5 times faster than the frontier models tested under identical serving conditions. The predecessor already runs inside Agentforce: Salesforce measured its routing response at roughly 26 milliseconds, compared with 1,446 milliseconds for a general frontier model. Anyone — including non-customers — can try the Koa Decisions demo after registering for a free Trailhead account. Planned integrations include Salesforce flows, Apex, prompt templates and a developer API.

Why it matters

The decision model is the quiet half of the agent story. Every agent today burns its smartest, slowest model on the easiest job in the pipeline: deciding what a request is, where it should go, and whether it is a yes or a no. That is a classification problem, not a reasoning problem — and a small, purpose-built model does it in milliseconds for a fraction of the cost. Koa Decisions joins the Koa family, which also includes a separate reasoning model built with NVIDIA for multi-step CRM work, already in pilot with customers including 1-800Accountant, Baxter Credit Union, Formula 1, UChicago Medicine and Xero, with general availability planned for winter 2026 in the US. The split is the strategy: a fast decision model routes and triages, a reasoning model works the hard cases. The honesty notes: the speed and accuracy figures are Salesforce's own measurements on its own benchmark, and the demo and HyperClassifier's existing deployment do not establish general availability for every planned integration — pricing, packaging and regional availability can still change.

What it means for you

If you run support, sales or operations, routing is the tax on every message — and a decision layer is the cheapest way to cut it. A model that triages, scores and sorts before a human or a big model sees the request can shrink queues, speed first responses, and keep the expensive model for the cases that actually need it. The practical move is the demo: a free Trailhead account is enough to test Koa Decisions on your own categories, before any Salesforce contract enters the picture. If you build agents, note the architecture lesson: put a decision model in front of your reasoning model, and measure the routing time — 26 milliseconds against 1.4 seconds is the difference between an agent that feels instant and one that feels like a queue. And if you are choosing AI tooling, watch the Koa rollout into flows, Apex and the developer API: that is when a Salesforce feature becomes a platform feature.

The Nodrits take

Salesforce's bet is that not every AI job needs reasoning — some need a fast yes or no. The Koa family splits the work deliberately: decide fast, reason when it counts. That is a healthier architecture than throwing a frontier model at every request, and it is also cheaper — which is why it will spread beyond Salesforce. The question for every team building agents is the same one Koa Decisions answers in 26 milliseconds: what is this request, and who should handle it?
