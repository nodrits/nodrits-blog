Toyota Is Building Waymo's Next Robotaxi: The Driverless Car Goes Factory-Built

November 1, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Toyota is building Waymo's next robotaxi — on its own production line. The two companies announced on October 30, 2026 that Toyota will manufacture the 2027 bZ4X electric SUV and integrate Waymo's self-driving system at its Takaoka plant in Japan, turning an 18-month-old exploration agreement into a factory-built product.

What happened

Under the announcement, Toyota will build the vehicles at its Takaoka plant in Toyota City, Aichi Prefecture, handling everything under one roof: assembling the base vehicle and installing Waymo's autonomous-driving hardware at the same site, instead of retrofitting finished cars elsewhere. The robotaxi bZ4X gets rider-focused changes — automatic doors, a power liftgate, generous rear legroom and ambient lighting — and runs Waymo's Level 4 system, which operates without a driver under defined conditions. Initial operations in 2027 will test and validate a small fleet in Japan, with deployment to the United Kingdom and other right-hand-drive Waymo markets planned for later years; no dates, volumes, prices or fleet sizes were disclosed. The deal follows a preliminary agreement signed in April 2025 to explore autonomous platform development, and Woven by Toyota, the group's software arm, is named as a strategic enabler. Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov pointed to Toyota's vehicle reliability and its commitment to zero traffic accidents.

Why it matters

This is the moment robotaxis stop being retrofits and start being products. A factory-integrated vehicle is cheaper to build at volume, easier to maintain, and designed for passengers from the start — which is exactly what a ride-hailing service needs to scale. For Japan, the companies call it a milestone toward the country's first fully autonomous ride-hailing service using high-volume, domestically manufactured vehicles. Waymo already plans fully driverless commercial rides in Tokyo in 2027 with taxi operator Nihon Kotsu and ride-hailing app GO, scaling to about 100 vehicles pending regulatory approval — and the bZ4X gives that service a purpose-built, right-hand-drive supply line. For the UK, where Waymo has been testing Jaguar vehicles in London, the bZ4X is the vehicle that could eventually carry a commercial service — though no UK date has been given, and the companies have not said whether the Toyotas will replace or supplement existing test vehicles.

What it means for you

If you run a business that depends on moving people or goods, the timeline that matters is permits, not press releases. Driverless services arrive city by city, approval by approval — so watch your local regulator, your insurer, and your curb space. Delivery and fleet operators should model a world where the per-mile cost of a driverless mile keeps falling, and where the companies that own the vehicle, the map and the rider relationship capture most of the value. And if you are hiring drivers today, plan retraining paths now: the transition will be gradual, but it will not reverse.
