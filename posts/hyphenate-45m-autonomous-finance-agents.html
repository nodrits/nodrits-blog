Hyphenate Raises $45M: The AI Agents Taking Over the CFO's Office

October 28, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The CFO's office just hired an AI — and it already does 90% of the routine work. New York startup Hyphenate, formerly Maximor, has raised a $45 million Series A to push its self-learning finance agents deeper into the office of the CFO, after a year in which its revenue grew 86 times and its customer base grew 11 times.

What happened

Hyphenate announced a $45 million Series A on October 27, 2026, led by Nexus Venture Partners, with participation from B Capital and existing investors Foundation Capital, BoldCap and Gaia Ventures, bringing total funding to $54 million after a $9 million seed round a year ago. The company, officially Prosperous Commerce Inc., builds an autonomous finance platform whose self-learning agents take over work across general ledger accounting, treasury management, order-to-cash, procure-to-pay and financial reporting, integrating with a company's existing ERP, accounting and banking systems rather than replacing them. The company says its agents now run 90% of routine finance work across the platform, and that customers report 90% less repetitive finance work and 75% fewer audit exceptions. Its customer base now includes six public companies, among them Groupon and Synthesia. One honest note: the 86x growth is from a small base a year ago, and the company describes its revenue only as multi-million-dollar ARR — the exact figure is undisclosed.

Why it matters

Finance is the hardest test for AI trust, because finance errors carry regulatory and reporting consequences that a chatbot apology cannot fix. Few CFOs have been willing to tolerate agents that might hallucinate a number into a filing. Hyphenate's pitch is built around that fear: agents prove one workflow to an auditor's standard, and then customers hand them the next one. The proof points the company cites are concrete — consumer goods firm Mini Melts USA recovered 16,640 hours of finance work a year within three months of going live on top of its SAP environment, and online auction platform HiBid cut its month-end close from three weeks to one day, with more than $2.5 billion in gross merchandise volume passing through the automated process. As co-founder and CEO Ramnandan Krishnamurthy put it: “Every finance team carries a long list of work it does because somebody has to.”

What it means for you

If you run finance, copy the pattern rather than the purchase: automate one workflow first — a reconciliation, an invoicing run — and demand auditor-grade accuracy before expanding. Keep humans on judgment calls and exceptions, and measure hours saved and audit exceptions, not tasks run. The round's lesson is not that AI is ready for finance in general; it is that finance teams hand over the next workflow only after the first one survives an audit.
