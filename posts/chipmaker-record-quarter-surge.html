AMD Smashes Records: Revenue Jumps 45% to $13.38 Billion as Data-Center Sales Nearly Double

November 4, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The chipmaker is AMD, and the shock is real: AMD reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $13.38 billion, up 45% year over year — a new quarterly record that beat Wall Street estimates. Its data-center business nearly doubled, and its forecast for the fourth quarter came in above every analyst estimate in sight.

What happened

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) announced its third-quarter 2026 results on November 3, 2026, after the US market close. Revenue reached $13.38 billion ($13,377 million), up 45% from $9.25 billion a year earlier and up 16% from the second quarter, beating the roughly $13.09 billion analysts expected. Adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share came in at $2.09, up 74% year over year and ahead of the $1.95 estimate; on a GAAP basis, net income rose 95% to $2.43 billion and diluted EPS rose 95% to $1.46. Data-center revenue jumped 97% to a record $8.55 billion, above the $8.20 billion estimate, while the client and gaming segment fell 8% to $3.72 billion, missing the $3.79 billion estimate. For the fourth quarter, AMD guided revenue to about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, above the $16.23 billion average analyst estimate, with a non-GAAP gross margin of about 55%.

Why it matters

The result lands right in the middle of the AI chip race. AMD says the shift toward running AI models in real-world applications — not just training them — is driving demand for its graphics processors and server CPUs, and it is gaining ground in its multibillion-dollar effort to challenge Nvidia's dominance of the AI semiconductor market. Shares rose more than 6% in extended trading, and Reuters notes the stock has more than doubled so far in 2026.

“Third quarter revenue grew 45% year-over-year to a record $13.4 billion, while non-GAAP earnings per share grew 74%, demonstrating the significant operating leverage in our business model as our Data Center business drives scale.” — Jean Hu, AMD executive vice president and chief financial officer
AMD also said its flagship Helios rack system, combining its MI455X accelerator and new “Venice” server chips, is in production with a larger ramp-up planned for the fourth quarter, and that initial systems are being tested at Meta — a customer that agreed in February to buy up to $60 billion of AI chips from AMD over five years.

What it means for you

For anyone building on AI, the message is that the chip supply story is no longer a one-company story. A second scaled supplier with record data-center demand means more capacity, more competition on AI hardware, and — over time — more choice for the cloud services and AI tools businesses rent every day. Watch the fourth quarter: AMD's own guide implies roughly 66% year-over-year growth, so the next report will show whether this acceleration holds or cools.
