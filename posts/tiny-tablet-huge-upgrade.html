Apple Unveils a New iPad Mini With an OLED Display and a Faster A20 Pro Chip

October 26, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Apple has unveiled a new iPad mini, and the headline upgrade is the screen: the smallest iPad now has a larger 8.5-inch OLED display, replacing the LCD panel of the previous model. It is powered by the new A20 Pro chip, starts at $699 for Wi-Fi and $849 for Wi-Fi + Cellular, and goes on sale November 6, with pre-orders opening October 28.

What happened

Apple announced the new iPad mini on Monday, October 26, 2026, just days before its “Pro and Tell” event on Wednesday. The tablet has an 8.5-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display — up from 8.3 inches, with bezels 24% thinner — in a thinner, lighter body just 5.4 mm thick, available in Midnight and Starlight. The OLED panel delivers up to 1,200 nits of peak brightness, more than twice the previous generation, 600 nits full-screen brightness for SDR and HDR content, and a 2,000,000:1 contrast ratio, with a nano-texture display option that cuts glare. The new model is also the first iPad mini with IP67 water and dust resistance, has a 12-megapixel Center Stage front camera, faster wireless and cellular connectivity, fast-charge support, and an “Acoustic Display” speaker system built into the screen. Pricing starts at $699 (Wi-Fi) and $849 (Wi-Fi + Cellular) for 128GB, rising to $999 for 512GB Wi-Fi; pre-orders open October 28 and availability begins November 6.

Why it matters

This is the biggest iPad mini update in years, and it matters beyond one small tablet: OLED — long reserved for Apple's Pro devices — is now reaching its smallest, most portable iPad, while the price rises $100 over the previous $599 starting price. MacRumors notes the hike is the model's second price increase this year, driven by memory and chip shortages, yet still below the $799 figure some reports had predicted. The move also lands days before Apple's “Pro and Tell” event, where more OLED Macs are expected, signalling that OLED is becoming the standard across Apple's lineup rather than a Pro-only luxury.

What it means for you

For readers, students and mobile professionals, the practical wins are the brighter, higher-contrast screen for reading and sketching outdoors, water resistance for a device that travels everywhere, and the faster chip for on-device Apple Intelligence features. The trade-off is the price: at $699, the mini now costs $100 more than its predecessor, and the nano-texture glass option is limited to the 256GB and 512GB models. If you already own the previous mini, the screen and water resistance — not raw speed — are the reasons to upgrade.
