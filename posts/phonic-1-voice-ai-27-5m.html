The Voice AI That Answers Before You Finish Talking: Phonic-1 Raises $27.5 Million

November 1, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The voice AI that answers before you finish talking is here. Phonic launched Phonic-1 on October 29, 2026 — a speech-to-speech model that starts searching for information and calling tools while the caller is still speaking — and announced a $27.5 million Series A to scale it.

What happened

Phonic-1 processes speech directly, without converting it to text first, and its headline trick is timing: instead of waiting for a pause, it begins the work — a search, a database lookup, a tool call — mid-sentence, so the answer lands almost as soon as the caller stops talking. Phonic says it responds in about 600 milliseconds; the independent Artificial Analysis speech-to-speech leaderboard lists Phonic 1 (High) at 0.61 seconds to first audio and an index score of 87.1, the top number on that page when read on October 29 — though phone-call latency in the wild has not been independently shown, and the claim that it is the only model calling tools while the caller speaks remains unverified by third parties. The $27.5 million Series A was led by First Round, with Lux Capital, Felicis and Definition participating, bringing Phonic's total funding to $31.5 million. The company says it now powers calls across six continents for customers including Ashby, Maven AGI, Rho and a Fortune 50 IT infrastructure company, with pricing from $0.07 per minute.

Why it matters

Voice agents have been held back by two things: latency that makes conversations feel robotic, and reliability that breaks on real-world speech — accents, interruptions, background noise. Phonic's bet is that solving both at the model level, rather than stitching together separate speech, language and voice models, is what finally makes AI phone agents trustworthy for customer support, job interviews and logistics. The funding round is a vote for that bet, from investors who have watched voice become the next interface after chat. The honest caveat: a benchmark lead is not a production guarantee, and the companies that win voice will be the ones whose agents complete tasks — bookings, resolutions, verifications — not just the ones that answer fastest.

What it means for you

If your business takes phone calls, test voice agents on your hardest calls, not your easiest demos: real accents, noisy lines, impatient callers who interrupt. Measure task completion — did the caller get what they needed? — not just response speed, and keep a clean human handoff for the cases the agent cannot close. Voice is becoming an AI-native interface, and the businesses that learn to supervise it early will set the standard their customers judge everyone else by.
