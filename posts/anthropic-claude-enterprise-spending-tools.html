Anthropic's New Claude Enterprise Tools Answer the Question Every CFO Is Asking: What Did the AI Actually Buy Us?

October 29, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Your company pays for AI by the token. Now it can see what it bought. Anthropic made three Claude Enterprise features generally available on October 28, 2026: smart reports that analyze what teams accomplish with Claude and what that work costs, in-app surveys that gather employee feedback, and pooled group budgets that let administrators set one shared monthly spending pool per team.

What happened

Anthropic announced the general availability of smart reports, in-app surveys and pooled group budgets for Claude Enterprise on October 28, 2026. Smart reports, in beta since September 10, let an administrator choose a team, one or more Claude applications and a reporting period of up to 28 days; they analyze a sample of that team's transcripts, categorize the tasks employees used Claude for, show what was produced, and calculate usage costs at Anthropic's standard pricing before discounts. They also flag issues such as unsuccessful tool calls and missing connectors, and suggest recurring workflows to turn into reusable skills. Each organization can generate 10 reports per month for free. Smart reports are disabled by default, and Anthropic says they are not intended for evaluating individual performance or making employment decisions. In-app surveys ask employees what Claude helped them achieve, how it affected their work time and what would slow down without it; administrators can edit the questions, select recipients and export answers. Pooled group budgets let a team share a monthly spending limit while retaining individual member limits — usage counts against both, is put on hold when either is reached, and administrators receive alerts at 50%, 75%, 95% and 100% of the team budget. Availability varies for organizations with HIPAA configurations or customer-managed encryption keys.

Why it matters

The launch lands against a background of growing pressure to demonstrate the efficiency of AI investments. AI budgets are now large enough for finance departments to audit, and the question has shifted from “does it work?” to “what, exactly, are we getting for this money?” Anthropic's own context, reported alongside the launch, is striking: its AI coding product Claude Code recorded an operating profit in the second quarter, and net revenue retention was reported at 900% — the same customers spending nine times more than a year earlier — while spending on agentic coding tools has grown roughly tenfold since the start of the year, per a Jellyfish survey of 276,000 developers cited in coverage. Retention figures and survey data are reported claims, not audited financials, but they explain the timing: the company that proves the value keeps the contract.

What it means for you

Measure value, not vibes. Track cost per outcome rather than prompts per person, fix the broken workflows the reports surface — failed tool calls and missing connectors are usually cheaper to fix than to work around — set a team budget before spending surprises you, and survey your team on the one question that matters: what would slow down without it?
