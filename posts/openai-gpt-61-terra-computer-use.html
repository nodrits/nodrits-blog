OpenAI Launches GPT-6.1 Terra: Computer-Use AI for About a Dollar a Task

October 27, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra, a model built for the unglamorous work that fills a workday: reading screens, filling in forms, logging invoices into accounting software, drafting proposals. It launched October 26, 2026 in ChatGPT Work and Codex and through the API — at $1 per million input tokens, half the price of GPT-6.1 Sol, with OpenAI claiming its agents complete tasks more than three times faster than Astra and Sol.

What happened

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra on October 26, 2026, as the successor to GPT-5.6 Terra in the middle of its lineup (Luna, Terra, Sol, Astra). The company describes it as a fast, efficient model pairing sharper visual reasoning and precise computer use with clearer, more natural writing. It is available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, and to developers through the API as gpt-6.1-terra. Standard API pricing is $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 per million cached input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, with a 1.05-million-token context window and 128,000 maximum output tokens.

Why it matters

This is computer-use AI priced for volume, not for demos. When a model can click, type and move between apps, the cost that matters is not per token but per completed task — and OpenAI's own figures put Terra at 50.3 percent on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum effort, up from 21.5 percent for GPT-5.6 Terra, with an internal hard-prompt error rate down to 4.3 percent at maximum effort. An honest caveat, stated plainly: these are OpenAI's own benchmarks, and independent evaluations were not out at launch. The 3x speed claim rests on OpenAI's own timing runs. Still, the direction is unmistakable — the race is shifting from the smartest model to the cheapest reliable worker.

What it means for you

Start with one repeatable screen task — invoice entry, form filling, a weekly report — and measure two numbers: success rate and cost per finished task. Keep a human approving anything that moves money or touches customer accounts, and never hand a brand-new agent live credentials on day one. If the task success rate holds for a month, widen the job; if it does not, the price never mattered.

The Nodrits take

Terra's real announcement is not a benchmark. It is a price list that makes all-day agent labour affordable for ordinary businesses — and a question every buyer should now ask of every AI vendor: what does a finished task cost, not a token?
