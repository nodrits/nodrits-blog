AI Just Found a Flaw Hiding in Most of the Web — It Sat There for 10 Years

October 22, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Some of the most dangerous software bugs are not new. They are old — sitting quietly in code the whole world depends on, waiting for someone (or something) sharp enough to notice them. This week, that something was AI.

What happened

Security researchers have disclosed two critical vulnerabilities in PHP — the language that powers roughly 70% of all websites, including WordPress and a huge share of business sites. The flaws, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and nicknamed "PHP2Shell", carry a CVSS severity score of 9.2 out of 10 and allow unauthenticated remote code execution — in plain terms, an attacker who never logs in can take over a vulnerable server.

Every PHP version since 5.4 is affected. The bugs are believed to have hidden in the codebase for over a decade. They were discovered with help from frontier AI models — GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 4 Argon — working alongside human researchers.

Why everyone is taking this seriously

Cloud security firm Wiz has described PHP2Shell as among the most severe flaws in software history, and the NSA has urged operators to patch immediately — or take affected systems offline.

Three things make this one different. First, scale: PHP runs most of the web, so the exposed surface is enormous. Second, access: no password or account is needed to exploit it. Third, speed: the same class of AI that helped find these flaws can also help attackers weaponize them — which compresses the safe window to patch from weeks into days.

What you should do

  • Patch now. Emergency fixes are available for PHP 8.2 and newer.
  • On an older PHP version? There is no patch coming — upgrading is the only fix.
  • Running WordPress or a PHP-based site? Ask your host or developer which PHP version your site runs, today.
  • Don't wait for symptoms. Exploitation of a flaw like this leaves little visible trace until the damage is done.

The bigger story

For years, the fear was that AI would write malware. PHP2Shell shows the other edge of the sword: AI reading millions of lines of mature code and spotting what a decade of human reviewers missed. Defenders got there first — this time. The race now is whether patching, auditing and defense can move at AI speed too.
