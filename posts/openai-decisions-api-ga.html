OpenAI's Decisions API Goes Public: AI Decisions in 100 Milliseconds

October 24, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

AI decisions just got roughly 10x faster. On Thursday, October 22, 2026, OpenAI opened its Decisions API to all customers — a "decision model" that cuts the wait between asking an AI to decide and getting the answer from 1–3 seconds down to 100–180 milliseconds. Instead of training a new model from scratch, OpenAI reconfigured its GPT-6 Luna model for one job: make a fast call and commit to it.

What happened

OpenAI first showed the Decisions API as a beta at its developer day on September 29, 2026. Three weeks later it went generally available to every customer. The API works differently from a standard chat-style API: developers supply questions plus a set of predefined answers, and the model returns probabilities, choices, or scores with confidence ratings. The canonical use cases are content classification, support-ticket routing, triage, and deciding what an AI agent should do next. The GA release also added voice and visual modalities plus geographically targeted inference. Early customers named include Box, Datadog, Ramp, and Speak — Speak, which builds AI language tutors, cut the time between its AI's actions from 1.4 seconds to 0.13 seconds.

Why it matters

This is the speed war becoming the AI race's new front line. A one-to-three-second pause kills voice conversations and real-time agent workflows; 100–180 milliseconds feels instant. As OpenAI API product lead Nikunj Handa put it: "The big unlock with Decisions API has been the speed, shrinking things from one to three seconds to 100, 120, 180 milliseconds." And of the model itself: "Right now, it's just plain old Luna that gets the job done." The backstory is striking: OpenAI is chasing a startup, TypeSafe, founded by a former OpenAI researcher, whose "Jev" model defined this category and hit a $7.6B valuation within weeks. Handa admitted his team saw Jev's traction — "we have like a million use cases for this" — and sprinted to respond. On public decision-model indices, OpenAI's model currently ranks 8th and 14th (rivals include Liquid's D1 Ultra, Base Lab's Elephant-1, and Jev) — and Handa's forward target is 60ms calls: "an API call for every single frame in a video."

What it means for you

If you run a business with support tickets, content queues, or approval workflows, decision models are the cheapest AI upgrade you'll make this year — not smarter answers, just faster ones, which is exactly what keeps live chat, voice agents, and triage pipelines feeling instant. For freelancers and agencies, it means real-time agent experiences (voice tutoring, live moderation, instant routing) move from "cool demo" to "shippable product." The practical takeaway: audit where your AI workflows stall on 1–3 second waits — that latency is now the problem being sold as the solution.

The Nodrits take

The most telling detail here is that OpenAI — the company that defined the AI race — had to sprint to catch a startup that was only weeks old. In AI infrastructure, the winner isn't the company with the biggest model anymore; it's the one with the fastest useful answer. And 60ms — one decision per video frame — is a genuinely different world: AI that can watch, react, and route in real time, everywhere.
