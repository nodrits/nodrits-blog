Daytona Raises $60M at $1B Valuation to Build a Cloud for AI Agents

October 23, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Daytona Platforms just became a unicorn. The three-year-old New York startup announced a $60 million Series B at a $1 billion valuation, led by FirstMark Growth — and the investor list reads like a who's who of tech: Stripe's Patrick Collison, Shopify's Tobi Lütke, Zoom's Eric Yuan, Datadog's Olivier Pomel, Perplexity's Aravind Srinivas, Quora's Adam D'Angelo (also an OpenAI board member), Mercor's Brendan Foody, plus Qualcomm Ventures, Samsung Next and Okta Ventures. Total funding now stands at $91 million.

What happened

Daytona announced the round on October 23, 2026. FirstMark also led the company's $24 million Series A back in February. Daytona's pitch: today's cloud was designed for long-lived services, but AI agents work differently — they spin up thousands of short, stateful jobs nobody planned in advance. So Daytona is building a cloud specifically for long-running agents: like giving every agent its own computer, with sandboxed Linux, Windows, macOS and GPU access on one platform.

Why it matters

This is a strong signal of where AI infrastructure money is moving. Model capabilities are commoditizing — the bottleneck is shifting to running agents reliably at scale. When the CEOs of Stripe, Shopify, Zoom and Datadog all write checks to the same dev-infrastructure startup, it's a bet that agentic workloads will need purpose-built infrastructure, not just bigger GPUs.

"Agents don't work like the long-lived services today's cloud was designed for. They spin up thousands of short, stateful jobs nobody planned in advance, and they need a cloud built for that." — Ivan Burazin, founder of Daytona

What it means for you

If you're building AI products or automations, the infrastructure conversation is changing: stateful, sandboxed environments per agent are becoming a first-class primitive. For businesses, this means AI agents will get cheaper to run and more reliable — the difference between a demo that impresses and a workflow that actually ships.
