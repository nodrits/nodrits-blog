Meta Buys Conductor — and Gives Its Users 16 Days to Leave

November 5, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Meta bought your AI coding team — and now it's shutting down. Meta acquired Conductor on November 4, 2026, the startup whose app lets developers run several AI coding agents at once — and in the same announcement, Conductor told its users their cloud workspaces have 16 days left.

What happened

Axios first reported the deal on November 4, 2026, and TechCrunch confirmed it the same day: Meta has acquired Conductor, formally Melty Inc., founded in 2024 by CEO Charlie Holtz and CTO Jackson de Campos, and the team will join Meta Superintelligence Labs, working with Nat Friedman and Alexandr Wang. Financial terms were not disclosed. Conductor's product, launched in July 2025, is a Mac app that runs coding agents in parallel: each task gets its own isolated workspace, with its own branch and git worktree, so several agents can work on the same codebase without treading on each other. The agents are not Conductor's own — the app runs first-party Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and OpenCode agents, with users bringing their own subscriptions and API keys — and a hosted version, Conductor Cloud, puts agents in sandboxed virtual machines that keep working for hours, checkable from an iPhone app. Conductor says more than 100,000 people use it, and it raised a $22 million Series A in March 2026 from Spark Capital and Matrix. The wind-down schedule is precise: new sign-ups and downloads stopped on November 4, Pro subscribers keep paid features free, Conductor Cloud becomes read-only on November 13, shuts down on November 20, and the company then begins deleting cloud user data. The local Mac app keeps working until early 2027, when the team promises something new.

Why it matters

The deal matters less as an acquisition than as a signal about where the coding-agent race moved. Conductor builds no models at all — its value is orchestration, the control room that lets a developer run a team of agents instead of chatting with one. Meta buying that layer, rather than another model lab, says the bottleneck in agentic coding is no longer the model; it is managing a team of them. The timing underlines it: on the same day, OpenAI announced it would acquire Pierre Computer, the code-storage startup behind part of Codex — the unglamorous machinery between a model writing code and a reviewer merging it. Both deals point at the same layer of the stack. And both carry the same warning for users: when a platform's strategy changes, the product you depend on can get a shutdown date, not a roadmap.

What it means for you

If you use Conductor Cloud, the action is immediate and non-negotiable: export everything before November 20, 2026, because deletion begins after shutdown, and read-only mode from November 13 means you can view and export but not send new messages. If you use the local Mac app, you have until early 2027, with new models and critical fixes promised in the meantime — but plan the migration now, not in January. If you are choosing agent tooling for a team, this week is a lesson in exit costs: prefer tools where your work lives in your own repositories and worktrees, not in a vendor's cloud, so an acquisition cannot strand your history.

The Nodrits take

Two agent-tooling acquisitions in a single day, and neither one bought a model. That is the story. The companies that will win agentic coding may not be the ones with the smartest single agent, but the ones that build the best way to run a team of them — and the users caught in between pay the migration cost every time a deal closes.
