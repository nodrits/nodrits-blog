Palantir Nearly Doubles Revenue to $2.31B as Business Customers Overtake Government Work for the First Time

November 3, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Palantir Technologies has nearly doubled its business in a single year. The data-analytics and AI software company reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $2.31 billion on Monday, November 2 — up 96% from $1.18 billion a year earlier and ahead of Wall Street's expectation of about $2.18 billion. And in a first for the company, revenue from American businesses overtook revenue from the US government.

What happened

Palantir reported the results after US markets closed on Monday. Adjusted earnings came in at 44 cents a share, beating the consensus estimate of about 41 cents, and the company reported a profit of $1.13 billion. US commercial revenue surged 148% to $984 million, edging past US government revenue of $953 million, which itself grew 96%. It was the 13th straight quarter in which Palantir's sales growth accelerated. Total contract value closed in the quarter was $2.92 billion, up just 6% from a year ago — the one soft spot in an otherwise blowout report. GAAP operating margin widened to 47%, up from 33% a year earlier.

Why it matters

The results matter far beyond one company's earnings call. Palantir, valued at roughly $442 billion, has become a bellwether for enterprise AI demand: its pitch is that businesses now want to control their own AI tools rather than rent intelligence from AI labs.

"We have now essentially doubled our entire business since the same quarter last year," CEO Alex Karp said in a letter to shareholders.
The raised outlook reinforces that demand story. Palantir lifted full-year 2026 revenue guidance to about $8.53 billion, up from $8.15–$8.16 billion and above consensus of roughly $8.21 billion, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $2.65 billion versus expectations near $2.46 billion.

What it means for you

For businesses, the milestone to watch is the commercial crossover. When a company built on government contracts starts earning more from ordinary businesses, it signals that AI data platforms have moved from experimental budgets into core operations. Companies evaluating AI vendors should note the flip side too: contract signings grew only 6%, so much of this quarter's surge came from existing customers expanding their use — a reminder to judge AI vendors on customer expansion, not just headline growth.

The Nodrits take

Palantir's quarter is the strongest evidence yet that enterprise AI spending is real, recurring revenue — not hype. The open question is whether growth this fast can survive its own valuation.
