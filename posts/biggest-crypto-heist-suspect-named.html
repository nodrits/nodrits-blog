FBI Names North Korea Behind the $6.3 Billion Binance Heist — the Biggest Crypto Theft in History

November 1, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The FBI has named the culprit: North Korea. The bureau said Saturday that the theft of more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance — the biggest crypto heist in history — was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking operation, and that the stolen funds are already being laundered.

What happened

On Thursday, October 29, attackers drained about $6.3 billion in bitcoin and other digital currencies from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, in roughly 20 minutes. On Saturday, October 31, the FBI said in a statement, together with the U.S. Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation division, that the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) was responsible for the theft, tied to "malicious cyber activity" by TraderTraitor. The bureau said it has observed TraderTraitor actors converting the stolen assets primarily into Ethereum, and expects further laundering through decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms and "instant swap services." It also published a list of wallet addresses linked to the operation and urged exchanges, blockchain analytics firms and other platforms to block transactions tied to them. Binance said Saturday it has set aside enough of the same digital currencies stolen to cover 90% of the missing assets — more than $6.34 billion in company funds — and promised customers will be fully reimbursed, with about $3 billion already frozen with industry help.

Why it matters

The attribution makes the Binance theft the largest known crypto heist on record — more than four times the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in February 2025, which the FBI also blamed on TraderTraitor. It also turns the incident into a live enforcement chase: because the stolen funds move on public blockchains, investigators can watch them in real time. As TRM Labs chief policy officer Ari Redbord put it: "It's bank robbery, in this case, at the speed of the Internet. However, we can also track and trace those funds in real time in order to build investigations, to disrupt and to seize back funds." For exchanges and crypto businesses, the FBI's address list is now an immediate compliance task — any platform that touches those wallets risks handling laundered funds.

What it means for you

If you hold crypto on an exchange, this is a reminder to check that withdrawals are working normally and to keep most holdings in a wallet you control. If you run a business that accepts crypto, screen incoming transactions against published sanctions and law-enforcement address lists — the FBI's TraderTraitor list is now part of that homework. And if you build or manage web platforms, treat API keys and hot-wallet credentials like the high-value targets this month's "Black October" hacking spree has proven them to be.
