A River Is About to Power the AI Cloud: Crusoe's 106-Megawatt Paraguay Bet

October 25, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The next big AI cloud may not be built in Virginia or Texas. It may be built beside a river in Paraguay. Crusoe, the AI infrastructure company, announced on October 22, 2026 that it will build high-performance AI compute infrastructure in Paraguay powered by the Itaipu Hydroelectric Dam — 106 megawatts of renewable power, with room to nearly triple.

What happened

Under a strategic partnership with Paraguayan energy and infrastructure developer Araico, Crusoe will build AI compute infrastructure adjacent to the Itaipu substation, on the Parana River between Brazil and Paraguay. The facility will tap directly into one of the largest and most reliable 100% renewable hydroelectric power sources in the world, and will serve both US cloud customers and growing demand across Latin America through Crusoe Cloud. Crusoe plans to bring an initial deployment online in mid-2027, with a path to scale to 106 MW of IT capacity and long-term options to expand to approximately 280 MW total. Araico will lead local development and site infrastructure; Crusoe will manage, operate and deliver the physical compute. Paraguay's President Santiago Pena welcomed the deal as a chance for Paraguay "to establish itself as a regional leader in AI infrastructure."

Why it matters

This is Crusoe's energy-first strategy in its purest form: locate AI compute directly at abundant energy sources, instead of hauling power to the compute. Data centers everywhere are hitting grid bottlenecks, permitting delays and rising power prices; sitting beside the dam's substation sidesteps transmission constraints and locks in renewable supply at scale. The deal also marks Crusoe's first dedicated AI cloud deployment in Latin America, extending a footprint that already spans North America and Europe — and it comes with high-level political backing, with US officials describing it as potentially the largest private foreign investment in Paraguayan history.

What it means for you

For anyone buying AI capacity, the lesson is geographic: cloud costs increasingly follow energy costs. Hydro- and solar-rich regions may soon host the cheapest AI capacity on the map, and sustainability claims will sit next to megawatt numbers in every procurement conversation. When you site AI workloads, ask where the power comes from — it may matter more than where the servers sit.
