This AI Claims It Can Predict What You'll Do: 42AI's $62.5M Behavioral World Model

November 3, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

This AI claims it can predict what you'll do. Israeli startup 42AI came out of stealth on November 2, 2026 with $62.5 million in seed funding and a behavioral world model it says can forecast how individual people will act — already running inside 10 financial brokerages.

What happened

TechCrunch reported the launch on November 2, 2026. 42AI, based in Tel Aviv, says its system estimates probabilities for how individual clients may act under changing circumstances, treating behavior as probabilistic rather than fixed — underlying psychological tendencies that persist even as circumstances change. The $62.5 million was not raised in a single splashy external round: CEO and co-founder Amit Gal-Or told TechCrunch it came from the founders and Infinity Group over three years of building in stealth. Infinity Group was founded by his father, Amir Gal-Or, who is also 42AI's chairman and a co-founder; Ehud Ezra is chief technology officer and a co-founder. The company says the model is already live at 10 large brokerages, generates more than seven figures in revenue (no precise figure given), and that one brokerage generated approximately $30 million in additional value through dynamic hedging — the company's claim, not an audited result. The approach draws on a preprint submitted to arXiv on February 19, 2026, describing a Large Behavioral Model fine-tuned to predict individual strategic choices from structured psychometric trait profiles, built with input from psychology, neuroscience and genetics experts.

Why it matters

The headline number needs its caveat, and to the company's credit, its CEO gave it. The claimed 98% accuracy does not mean calling every individual trade correctly; it means trading flows landing inside a predicted range. That distinction is the whole story of this launch: a model that predicts distributions of human behavior is genuinely useful to a brokerage managing risk, and genuinely different from a crystal ball. It also explains why the next markets 42AI names — gaming, fraud prevention, cybersecurity, and eventually robots and AI agents that understand people — should make everyone pause. A model that knows why you act, not just what you bought, is a new kind of leverage, and the question of who holds it, with what consent, will matter more than the accuracy figure.

What it means for you

If you work with customer data, this launch is a prompt to audit your own position: what behavioral signals do you already collect, who can see them, and what would change if a model could explain your customers back to you? If you are a customer, the question is simpler and sharper: would you want a company to know not just what you did, but why — and what would it have to offer you in return? Accuracy without consent is surveillance with better marketing, and the companies that win this category will be the ones that answer the consent question first, not last.
