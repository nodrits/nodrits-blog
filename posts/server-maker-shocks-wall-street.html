Super Micro Smashes Expectations: $19.95 Billion Revenue as AI Server Demand Surges

November 4, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The AI server giant is Super Micro Computer. The San Jose company reported first-quarter revenue of $19.95 billion for the quarter ended September 30 — far above the $16.39 billion analysts expected — and then raised its full-year forecast, betting the AI infrastructure boom still has a long way to run.

What happened

Super Micro forecast second-quarter revenue of $19.5 billion to $21.5 billion, above the $17.14 billion Wall Street estimate, according to Reuters. It earned $1.81 per share on an adjusted basis, compared with estimates of $1.23, and gross margin came in at 12.7%, ahead of its preliminary estimate of 12.0% to 12.5%. For fiscal 2027, the company now expects revenue of $72.0 billion to $82.0 billion, up from its prior forecast of $65.0 billion to $72.0 billion and above the Street estimate of $69.17 billion. The company also appointed former Western Digital executive Mark Long as chief financial officer last month, ending a search that had run for nearly two years.

Why it matters

Super Micro has been one of the clearest beneficiaries of the AI infrastructure boom. Reuters notes strong demand for its Nvidia-powered systems as cloud providers and technology companies keep expanding data-center capacity. When a server maker beats revenue estimates by more than $3.5 billion in a single quarter and lifts its full-year outlook by billions, it signals that AI data-center spending is not slowing — it is accelerating. That is good news for the whole AI hardware supply chain, from chipmakers to cooling and power suppliers, and it puts pressure on rivals to keep pace on delivery times and capacity.

What it means for you

For businesses, the message is practical: AI infrastructure capacity is still the bottleneck, and the companies building it are scaling fast. If your business plans to run AI workloads, expect server lead times and data-center costs to stay front of mind, and plan capacity early rather than assuming hardware will be available on demand. For investors and tech watchers, Super Micro has become a useful barometer of real AI demand — its order book reflects what cloud providers are actually buying, not just what they announce.
