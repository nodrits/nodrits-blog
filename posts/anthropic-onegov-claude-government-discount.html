Claude Goes to Every Level of Government: Anthropic's 40% OneGov Deal Explained

November 2, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Claude goes to every level of government. Anthropic and Carahsoft announced on October 30, 2026 an expanded agreement through the US General Services Administration's OneGov programme, giving eligible agencies 40% off Claude through January 31, 2029 — and, for the first time, opening the deal beyond federal buyers to state, local, tribal and territorial governments.

What happened

Under the agreement, eligible agencies receive a 40% discount off list price on Claude, with no minimum spend commitment and no large upfront commitment. Agencies pay for actual usage, billed monthly in arrears, up to an amount each agency sets for itself — a consumption model, not a seat licence. Eligible organizations can select Claude for Government, Claude Enterprise, the Claude API, or a combination. Access is transactable through Carahsoft's GSA Multiple Award Schedule, and OneGov pricing applies to purchases through GSA MAS and the California Multiple Award Schedules. The deal replaces a promotional arrangement under which federal agencies could use Claude's enterprise and government products for $1 per user; that offer ended on October 31, 2026. Claude for Government has been generally available since September 30 and runs at the FedRAMP High level, according to Anthropic, which puts its expected savings from the agreement at about $1 billion — the company's own projection, not an audited result.

Why it matters

Government AI procurement has quietly become one of the largest enterprise AI markets, and its terms now set signals for everyone else. The shift from a $1 headline price to metered, discounted usage is the market maturing: agencies buy tokens the way they buy electricity, with caps and monthly billing. The competitive context matters too — OpenAI's own OneGov agreement, reported at a 50% discount over 27 months, took effect at the start of October — so the two leading labs are now in a visible price race for the public sector. When governments negotiate like bulk buyers, the terms they extract — usage-based pricing, no minimums, agency-set caps — tend to spread into enterprise contracts next.

What it means for you

If you sell to the public sector, expect AI pricing, data-handling and security questions in every tender from here on, and be ready to answer them with the same specifics: discount, meter, cap, security level. If you buy AI privately, watch government terms as an early signal of where enterprise floors are heading — and borrow the discipline: budget by usage with caps rather than flat seats, and keep a human in the loop for anything public-facing.

The Nodrits take

Governments don't buy AI like consumers. They buy it like a utility — metered, capped and negotiated. That is a sign of a market growing up. Should governments get a discount on AI, or pay the same price as everyone else? Tell us in the comments on today's post.
