Disruptive Raises $10B Fund to Back at Least 10 AI and Defense Companies

October 28, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Dallas-based investment firm Disruptive has announced a $10 billion fund to back at least 10 late-stage companies, with checks of up to $1 billion each. It is one of the biggest fundraises of the year so far — and it is aimed squarely at the two most capital-hungry fields in tech right now: AI and defense.

What happened

Disruptive announced the fund on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, TechCrunch reported. The vehicle will target late-stage companies, and the firm says it plans to write checks of up to $1 billion. The Wall Street Journal had first reported earlier in October that the firm was seeking the fund, and that it had already secured at least $7.5 billion in capital commitments by then. The new fund is also, according to the Dallas Business Journal, the largest venture fund ever raised in Texas history.

Why it matters

The structure matters as much as the size. This is technically Disruptive’s first blind-pool fund, meaning investors committed money without knowing which companies it will back. Until now, the firm raised money deal by deal through special purpose vehicles. That investors still lined up for a $10 billion blind pool says a lot about where capital believes the next wave of AI and defense winners will come from — and about Disruptive’s track record: Bloomberg reported the firm told limited partners it has returned more than $6 billion on roughly $1.9 billion poured into startups. Its past bets include Palantir, Airbnb and Spotify, and more recently chip startup Groq and AI voice startup ElevenLabs.

What it means for you

For founders, the bar for late-stage AI and defense capital just moved again: the biggest checks are concentrating in fewer, larger funds. For everyone else watching the AI build-out, a $10 billion bet placed blind is a loud signal that investors still expect enormous companies to emerge from AI infrastructure and defense technology — even as markets grow nervous about how the AI boom is financed. The firm was founded in 2012 by Alex Davis, grandson of billionaire oil magnate Marvin Davis, and its shift from deal-by-deal investing to a single giant pool marks a new chapter for the Dallas firm.

The Nodrits take

A $10 billion blind pool is either a vote of confidence or a sign of how frothy AI and defense investing has become — possibly both.
