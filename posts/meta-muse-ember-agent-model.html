Meta Releases Muse Ember, Its Lightest AI Model Built for Agents That Work All Day

November 9, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Meta just launched the smallest member of its Muse family — and it may be the most important one for real-world AI agents. Muse Ember, released on Monday, November 9, 2026 by Meta's Superintelligence Lab, is the lab's lightest and most cost-efficient AI model yet, designed specifically for the repetitive execution work agents do all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long document parsing. At 10 cents per million input tokens, Meta says Ember is cheaper and smarter than rival lightweight models like Anthropic's Haiku and OpenAI's Luna — and cost-competitive with open-source options like DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash.

What happened

Meta's Superintelligence Lab announced Muse Ember on November 9, 2026. In its release, Meta described Ember as built for "complex reasoning execution and the work agents repeat all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction, and long document parsing." The emphasis is squarely on cost-per-task: using Artificial Analysis's composite benchmark score, Meta positioned Ember as cheaper and smarter than Anthropic's Haiku and OpenAI's Luna, while also matching open-source competitors on price.

Ember is designed to pair with a bigger model rather than replace one. Meta's framing is that the heavy lifting of planning goes to its frontier model, Muse Wonder, while Ember handles the volume — as Meta puts it, "Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume." In a customer-support agent, for example, a heavier model might establish the rules for handling an issue, then hand the rules to Ember to apply across tens of thousands of cases.

Ember is also multimodal by design. Meta trained it to work across text, images and audio, so agents can read screenshots, charts and scanned documents, or work from voice memos, meeting recordings and video without switching to a separate model. It is the fourth model released under the Muse brand, following the mid-weight Spark in April, Spark's open-source twin Glimmer in August, and the Wonder frontier model in October. The flagship consumer product, the Muse Agent, launched in September.

Why it matters

The AI race is shifting from who builds the smartest model to who builds the cheapest model that is smart enough. Frontier models win benchmarks; lightweight models win budgets. Every agent that runs all day — answering support tickets, triaging documents, routing requests, running coding subtasks — multiplies its cost by thousands of calls, and at that scale a few cents per million tokens is the difference between a product that ships and a product that stays a demo. Ember is Meta's bet that the agent era will be won on cost-per-task, not just capability.

It also tightens the Muse family into a complete stack: Wonder plans, Spark handles the middle, Ember does the volume, and Glimmer gives the open-source community the same shape to build on. For Meta, that is a direct challenge to Anthropic and OpenAI in the small-model tier, where most real agent deployments actually live.

What it means for you

If you run a business that uses AI agents — support automation, document processing, coding assistants — the cost of running agents all day is about to drop sharply, and models like Ember are why. The practical play is a two-tier setup: a strong model for planning and judgement, a cheap model for the repetitive execution. That pattern is exactly how Meta describes Ember working with Wonder, and it is a pattern any team can copy with today's tools. For freelancers and small teams, cheap-but-capable models mean agent workflows that were too expensive to run continuously are now affordable to leave on.
