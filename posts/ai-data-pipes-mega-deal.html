Marvell Buys Lumentum in a $115 Billion Bet on AI's Optical Future

November 2, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Marvell Technology is buying Lumentum in a $115 billion all-stock deal, including debt — one of the biggest acquisitions of 2026 and a direct challenge to Broadcom in the race to wire up AI data centers. Under the agreement announced on Monday, November 2, 2026, Lumentum shareholders will receive 4.5 Marvell shares for each share they own, giving them roughly 33% of the combined company, with the deal expected to close in the first half of calendar 2028.

What happened

Marvell will pay $1,132 per share in stock, a 23.9% premium to Lumentum's last unaffected closing price on October 28, before deal talks were first reported — an equity value of $102.54 billion. The transaction was unanimously approved by both companies' boards and still needs shareholder and regulatory approvals. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy will remain chairman and CEO of the combined company, which Marvell says will have a $550 billion total addressable market. Lumentum makes photonic products, including high-performance lasers and optical components used in AI, cloud computing and communications networks. Separately, Lumentum said it expects second-quarter revenue of $1.58 billion to $1.63 billion, above analysts' average estimate of $1.48 billion, according to LSEG data.

Why it matters

The deal is a bet that AI's next bottleneck is not computing power but moving data between thousands of chips at ever-higher speeds. "AI infrastructure is undergoing a fundamental architectural shift toward optical connectivity, creating an opportunity that comes along once in a generation," Murphy said. "We intend to help shape the next era of AI connectivity and create exceptional stockholder value." The move also sharpens Marvell's rivalry with Broadcom, long Alphabet-owned Google's main custom-chip partner. But the market reaction was cautious: Lumentum shares fell 1.5% and Marvell 0.7% before the bell, and both stocks have fallen sharply since talks leaked in late October, as investors question whether AI spending is sustainable. "This is an enormous bet, and investors are right to ask whether Marvell is paying too much and taking on too much at once," said Axel Rudolph, chief technical analyst at IG Group. Nvidia, which invested $2 billion in each company earlier this year, did not immediately comment.

What it means for you

For anyone building on AI infrastructure, the message is that connectivity is becoming as strategic as compute itself: faster, more energy-efficient optical links decide how big AI clusters can grow. Marvell has been building toward this for months — it closed a $3.5 billion purchase of Celestial AI in February, and in August signed a deal to supply chips alongside Google's AI processors worth up to $120 billion in sales through fiscal 2033, with Google holding the right to buy a $12.2 billion stake. At its October investor day, Marvell forecast fiscal 2031 sales of $70 billion to $90 billion, with about $37.5 billion from interconnect — a market it expects to grow 65% a year to $65 billion by 2030. Lumentum, which competes with Coherent and China's Zhongji Innolight, could also benefit from a proposed US ban on new Chinese optical transceivers that would shift demand toward domestic suppliers.

The Nodrits take

This is Marvell's largest purchase since its $10 billion buyout of Inphi in 2021, and it turns a once little-known chipmaker — market value now above $170 billion, annual revenue more than doubled from $3 billion in fiscal 2021 to $8.2 billion in fiscal 2026 — into a full-stack AI connectivity player. The risk is execution: a $115 billion integration, years from closing, in a market already asking hard questions about the AI boom.
