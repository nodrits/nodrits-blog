AvalonX Emerges From Stealth With $10M: AI Hackers for the Small Businesses That Could Never Afford Them

October 29, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Small businesses get hacked like enterprises. Now they can fight back. AvalonX, an AI-powered offensive security startup, emerged from 18 months in stealth on October 28, 2026 with up to $10 million in funding from Ridown Capital — and a platform that lets small and midsize businesses attack themselves, continuously, before real attackers do.

What happened

AvalonX Inc., headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia, announced its public launch in October 2026 after 18 months in stealth, backed by up to $10 million from Ridown Capital, the investment arm of Ridown Group, led by Aaron Brown and Darren Ridge. Its Artemis platform, powered by the proprietary Aether security AI trained on nine years of real-world offensive-security data, combines autonomous testing agents with human verification to deliver continuous, on-demand penetration testing that small and midsize businesses can afford, sold as a monthly subscription. The company is led by cybersecurity veterans Craig Goodwin, Ricardo Valdes and Ben O'Nion, who bring more than 50 years of combined information security experience. AvalonX is available now, and businesses can run a free initial test at avalonx.ai. Ridown's backers frame the bet through their other business, Onecom, which serves more than 65,000 small and midsize businesses: “They're targeted like enterprises but resourced like startups,” said Brown.

Why it matters

Most smaller firms rely on periodic penetration tests, leaving long gaps while systems and threats keep evolving. Verizon's 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report, cited in Dealroom's coverage, found ransomware in 88% of breaches involving small and midsize companies, compared with 39% at larger organizations — the gap between being tested constantly and being tested once a year is the gap attackers walk through. AvalonX enters a crowded field — Dutch startup Hadrian raised $40 million in October for its AI-agent platform for finding exploitable weaknesses, and French startup Fleuret AI announced a €4 million pre-seed for automated testing — and its announcement alone does not prove differentiation. The honest read: the pitch is affordability, but the proof will be renewals after the first scare.

What it means for you

Run the free first test before you buy anything, fix the breach paths the test finds in order of exploitability, and re-test after every big system change. A yearly pen test is a snapshot, not a shield — and for a small business, the cheapest breach is the one your own AI found first.
