Apple Intelligence Finally Arrives in China: iOS 27.3 Brings AI to iPhones in December

October 31, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

After a wait of more than two years, Apple Intelligence is finally coming to mainland China. Apple confirmed in a press release on its China newsroom that the first wave of Apple Intelligence features will open for testing with the iOS developer beta on November 4, 2026, and will reach all users with the public release of iOS 27.3 in December. Alibaba's Qwen AI will power some of the experience — but one major feature is still missing.

What happened

Apple's announcement, published on its China newsroom and reported by MacRumors and 9to5Mac on October 30, 2026, confirms the timeline: testing begins November 4 with the next iOS developer beta (November 3 in the United States, due to the time-zone difference), and the public launch follows in December with iOS 27.3. Apple has not given an exact December date. The first rollout is iPhone-only, covering the iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, iPhone 16 and later models, iPhone Air and iPhone Duo. Both Siri and the device language must be set to English or Simplified Chinese. The first feature set includes Writing Tools, Genmoji, Clean Up in Photos, natural-language search in Photos, Memory Movies, Visual Intelligence, notification summaries, Priority Notifications, and Live Translation in Messages, FaceTime and Phone, plus live face-to-face translation through supported AirPods. Apple says it has partnered with Alibaba so users in China can use Alibaba's Qwen directly within Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence for certain text- and image-understanding requests. Support for Mac, iPad and other devices will follow only after further regulatory approvals, Apple said.

Why it matters

China is one of Apple's largest markets, and the absence of Apple Intelligence there — since the platform first shipped outside China in October 2024 — has been one of the company's most conspicuous gaps. The delay was regulatory as much as technical: Apple needed local approvals and a local AI partner, and earlier reporting by Bloomberg in October 2026 said the December launch had been planned with guardrails developed with Alibaba. Apple's confirmation turns a reported plan into an official commitment, and it makes Alibaba's Qwen a visible part of the iPhone experience for hundreds of millions of users.

"Apple has reached a partnership with Alibaba to add support for Qwen in certain Apple Intelligence experiences." — Apple, China newsroom press release
The other side of the announcement matters just as much: Siri AI and the other next-generation Apple Intelligence features announced at WWDC 2026 are not part of the China release, and Image Playground will not be available in China either, according to MacRumors. China gets the established 2024 feature set, not the newest AI.

What it means for you

If you use an iPhone in mainland China, check two things when December arrives: your model must be iPhone 15 Pro or newer, and your Siri and device languages must be set to English or Simplified Chinese. Developers can test from November 4 in the developer beta. For businesses selling to Chinese customers, the arrival of Writing Tools, Visual Intelligence and Live Translation on iPhones changes what on-device AI can do in everyday apps — but if your plans assumed the newest Siri AI, reset them: that feature is not in this release, and Apple has given no date for it in China.

The Nodrits take

Two years is a long time in AI, and Apple arriving late in China with the older feature set — while leaning on a local partner for part of the brain — says a lot about how AI is splitting into regional versions. The features landing in December are genuinely useful, but the missing Siri AI is the real story: the most advanced version of Apple's AI still has no date in its second-biggest market.
