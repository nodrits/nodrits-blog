The Biggest Open AI Brain Ever Just Went Free: Kimi K3.1 Pro's 2.8 Trillion Weights

November 5, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The AI brain too big to download just went free. Moonshot AI published the full weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face on November 4, 2026 — 2.8 trillion parameters, 96 files, roughly 1.56 terabytes — making it the largest open-weight model ever released.

What happened

Moonshot AI launched Kimi K3.1 Pro on October 23, 2026, calling it its most capable 3T-class open-weight model to date, and promised the weights by November 4. The company kept that promise: the repository moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro appeared on Hugging Face — created on November 2, downloadable without an access request — and news of the release spread widely on November 4. The model is a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts system: 2.8 trillion total parameters, approximately 104 billion active per token, 896 experts with 16 selected per token, a 1,048,576-token context window, and weights stored at 4-bit precision (MXFP4) after quantization-aware training. In 24 of its 93 layers, full Gated MLA attention is replaced by sparse Gated DSA (DeepSeek Sparse Attention), accelerating processing for million-token contexts. Deployment recipes for vLLM and SGLang are published, and the model remains available via API as kimi-k3.1-pro at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with $0.20 cached input. The licence is Moonshot's own custom text, the Kimi K3.1 Pro License: free to use, change and sell, but a model-as-a-service business with more than $20 million in revenue over 12 months needs a separate agreement, and products above 100 million monthly users or $20 million monthly revenue must show the model's name.

Why it matters

Two things make this release bigger than its parameter count. First, the capability claim: Moonshot's own table puts K3.1 Pro at 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, within a point of Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4% and ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 58.2%, with 94.2 on GPQA-Diamond, 92.1 on BrowseComp and 79.6 on OSWorld V2.1 — and independent evaluator Artificial Analysis ranks it fourth among all models it scores. Second, the safety edit: Moonshot says it traced rising exploit skill to cyber content in its general mid-training corpus and reinforcement-learning environments, and removed both before opening the weights. The catch is the hardware bar. The vLLM recipe runs the model across eight GPUs — two Nvidia GB300 trays, one B300 node or one AMD MI355X node — so for most teams local deployment is a data-center project, not a download. And the benchmark figures are vendor-reported: no independent lab has yet rerun the tests on the downloadable files.

What it means for you

For most teams, the honest way to use K3.1 Pro this week is the API, not the weights: $2 in and $10 out is a third cheaper than K3, and it buys the same model without a cluster. If you do plan to self-host, budget for the cluster first and read the licence before building a business on it — the $20 million model-as-a-service threshold is a real constraint. If you evaluate models, treat Moonshot's scores as a starting point: the weights are public now, which means independent verification is possible for the first time, and the community's reruns will matter more than the launch table. And if you run a lab, note the precedent: a frontier model shipping with its hacking skills deliberately removed before publication is a safety norm worth copying.

The Nodrits take

A 2.8-trillion-parameter open model narrows the gap between public and private labs — but the hardware bar keeps the weights in few hands. Openness that only data centers can use is still progress, just uneven progress. The real test of this release is not the download count. It is whether independent labs verify the scores, and whether anyone outside a data center finds a use for a brain this big.
