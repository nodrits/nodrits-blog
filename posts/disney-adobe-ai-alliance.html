Disney and Adobe Team Up to Build Custom AI Models Trained on Disney's Own Worlds

November 9, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Disney just handed its magic to AI — on its own terms. The Walt Disney Company and Adobe announced a multi-year strategic alliance on Monday, November 9, 2026, at Adobe MAX in Miami Beach: the two companies will co-develop custom generative AI models trained exclusively on Disney's own intellectual property, with Adobe named Disney's Official AI Innovation Partner.

What happened

Under the alliance, Disney and Adobe will build generative AI models through Adobe Firefly Foundry — Adobe's platform for enterprises that want custom models built on their own material. The models will be trained on Disney's characters, worlds, environments and visual styles, and will stay private and proprietary to Disney, producing what Adobe calls production-ready outputs. Adobe will also give Disney early access to its latest creative and AI technologies — Adobe Firefly, Firefly Foundry, Premiere and Photoshop — along with dedicated engineering and research resources to support Disney's production pipeline.

The work spans Disney's whole empire. Disney Experiences — the division behind its theme parks, resorts and cruise lines — will use the tools to speed up project development timelines. The Walt Disney Studios' innovation hub, StudioLAB, will use them for concept exploration. And ESPN Edge will use AI tools to boost engagement with sports fans and bring more of ESPN's content archive to audiences.

This is not starting from zero. Walt Disney Imagineering Research & Development has already been using Adobe Firefly Foundry since June 2026, including a sketch-to-image model that turns hand-drawn concepts into fully rendered 2D concept art and a custom image model that generates on-brand, franchise-accurate creative assets. The new alliance scales that work across the company. Neither company disclosed financial terms.

Why it matters

This is the biggest entertainment company on the planet putting its most valuable asset — a century of characters and stories — into generative AI, but only inside a walled garden it controls. The training-data question has been the central fight in creative AI: models trained on scraped work, lawsuits, and artists worried about being copied. Disney's answer is a private model trained only on Disney's own library, with outputs that stay inside Disney. If it works at Disney's scale, it becomes the template every major studio and brand will be asked to follow: don't ban AI, own the model.

For Adobe, it is a flagship win for Firefly Foundry — proof that the biggest creative enterprise in the world trusts its platform to build proprietary models. And for the creative industries, it moves AI from an experiment at the edges into the production pipeline of films, theme parks and sports broadcasting.

What it means for you

If you work in design, media or marketing, the message is that custom, private AI models trained on your own brand assets are becoming the standard — not generic tools trained on the open internet. The competitive edge is shifting from who has the best AI tool to who has the best proprietary data to train it on. For small businesses and creators, the same idea scales down: your own photos, your own style, your own voice, in a model that works for you and nobody else.
