Pennsylvania Just Made AI Ads Confess: Labels Required From December 21

October 25, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Pennsylvania just became one of the first states in America to force AI-generated advertising to identify itself. Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act on October 22, 2026 — and from December 21, any ad that uses AI-generated or significantly modified content in a way that could mislead consumers must carry a clear label, or its maker faces enforcement.

What happened

The law, Senate Bill 806, now Act 99 of 2026, passed the Senate 48-2 on June 10 and the House 163-40 on October 7, and was signed on October 22 as one of 58 bills Shapiro approved that day. It takes effect 60 days after signing, on December 21, 2026. The rule targets advertisements whose images, video or audio were generated or significantly modified by AI in a way that creates a false consumer perception about the nature, origin or authenticity of the goods or services advertised. When triggered, the ad must include a clear and conspicuous disclosure, shown when the synthetic content is first presented, in the same medium as the content, and — where technically feasible — permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove.

Why it matters

The law's reach is what makes it nationally relevant. It applies regardless of where an advertisement was produced: a commercial cut in California and aired in Philadelphia carries the same obligation, and it covers everyone from multi-billion-dollar brands to the small restaurant printing flyers. That is why compliance teams expect spillover — national advertisers are likely to standardize labels everywhere rather than geofence Pennsylvania. Enforcement sits with the state attorney general and district attorneys, who may seek injunctions and civil penalties of up to $3,000 plus legal fees and costs. The law exempts text-only content and material that is evidently fictional, parody, satire, commentary or criticism — but leaves key terms such as "significantly modified" and "false consumer perception" open, which lawyers say will be tested in practice.

What it means for you

If you run ads anywhere in the US, audit your AI-generated assets now: synthetic people, synthetic product shots, synthetic voices. Put the disclosure in the same format as the ad itself — on screen for video, on the page for print — not buried in a footnote. December 21 is closer than your next campaign cycle, and the cheapest compliance strategy is labeling early rather than litigating what "significantly modified" means later.
