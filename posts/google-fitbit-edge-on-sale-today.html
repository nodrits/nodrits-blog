Google Fitbit Edge Goes on Sale Today: The Fitness Band Is Back

October 27, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The fitness band is back — and it goes on sale today. Google's Fitbit Edge, priced at $179.99, hits shelves on October 27, 2026, bringing smartwatch-grade sensing and a Gemini-powered health coach to a slim band that lasts up to a week on a charge. The catch, as The Verge's review notes, is that the coach itself is a subscription.

What happened

Google announced the Fitbit Edge on October 12, 2026, and general availability begins today, October 27, with pre-orders already open through the Google Store and retail partners. The Verge published its review on October 26. The device sits between a screenless band and a full smartwatch: a 1.34-inch hybrid OLED display in an 11.5mm recycled-aluminum case, built-in GPS, altimeter, ECG, SpO2 and skin-temperature sensing, more than 50 exercise modes, and 5 ATM / IP68 water resistance. A Stephen Curry Special Edition costs $219.99.

Why it matters

Wearables have moved past step counting: the product now is guidance. Through the Google Health app — which replaces the old Fitbit app experience — the Edge offers sleep coaching, cycle tracking, nutrition and metabolic guidance, and plain-language summaries of lab reports, with the Gemini-powered Health Coach answering questions about your results. The trade-offs are deliberate: no wrist calls and no contactless payments, because, Google told The Verge, fewer than one percent of Charge 6 owners used payments. The battery story is the other headline: up to seven days per charge (two days with the always-on display), a full charge in about 90 minutes, and a five-minute charge for roughly a day of use.

What it means for you

Budget the subscription, not just the band: every buyer gets three months of Google Health Premium included, after which The Verge reports it costs $10 a month. Check ECG availability in your country before buying, and be honest about what you want — if you want a distraction-free tracker with a coach, this is the pitch; if you want a mini phone on your wrist, look at a full smartwatch instead.

The Nodrits take

Google is betting that people will pay monthly for a coach, not just once for hardware. That only works if the coaching stays genuinely useful week after week — the hardware is the ticket in, and the subscription is the business.
