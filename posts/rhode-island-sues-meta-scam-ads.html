Rhode Island Sues Meta Over Scam Ads — Internal Documents Cite Billions

October 22, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

A US state has sued Meta Platforms for the first time over its alleged tolerance of scam advertising. Rhode Island Attorney General Peter Neronha filed the suit on Thursday in Providence Superior Court, accusing Meta of unlawfully profiting from — and participating in the creation of — billions of dollars in scam ads across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, even while assuring users it was working to protect them.

What happened

Rhode Island sued Meta Platforms (META.O) in state court on Thursday, October 22, 2026, over the company's alleged tolerance for widespread scam advertising on its Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp platforms. The suit, filed by Attorney General Peter Neronha in Providence Superior Court, accuses Meta of profiting from and participating in the creation of billions of dollars in scam advertising. It is the first such scams-related case brought against Meta by a US state, following a similar lawsuit filed in May by California's Santa Clara County. The lawsuit seeks monetary civil penalties for each alleged violation of Rhode Island state law, restitution to consumers, disgorgement of ill-gotten gains, and a court order requiring the company to address — rather than profit from — the scam ads it delivers.

Why it matters

The case leans on Reuters reporting from last year based on internal Meta documents, which outlined the vast scale of fraudulent advertising on the company's platforms — and Meta's own calculations that it was earning far more from high-risk scam ads than it expected to pay in fines for allowing them. According to those documents, Meta displays around 15 billion "higher risk" scam ads to users every day, and earned roughly 10% of its 2024 revenue from fraudulent e-commerce, dubious health products and other banned marketing.

"As alleged, Meta knowingly exposed vulnerable users to fraud and scams, and made enormous profits in doing so. When companies, big or small, take advantage of Rhode Island consumers, this office will do everything within our authority to hold them to account." — Rhode Island Attorney General Peter Neronha

What it means for you

If the allegations hold, a significant share of the ads in your social feeds may have been ones Meta's own systems flagged as risky. The suit also puts a spotlight on ad-review practices across the industry — expect more scrutiny of how platforms balance ad revenue against user safety, and potentially stricter verification rules for advertisers.

The Nodrits take

Platforms built on ad revenue policing their own ads has always been a conflict of interest — this lawsuit puts real numbers on it.
