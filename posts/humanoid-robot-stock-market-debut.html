First Humanoid Robot Company Hits the Stock Market — Agility Robotics Lists on Nasdaq

November 5, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Agility Robotics is going public. The maker of the Digit humanoid robot is listing on the Nasdaq through a reverse-merger with a special purpose acquisition company (SPAC), raising $465 million in new capital from retail investors and a tranche of private funding — making it the first pure humanoid robot company on the stock market.

What happened

Agility announced its market debut on November 5, 2026, with trading under the ticker AGLT following the closing of its combination with Churchill Capital XI. The deal was announced on October 30 and is expected to bring in roughly $465 million in gross capital. CEO Peggy Johnson told TechCrunch ahead of the debut that the company deliberately avoided the flashy demos of its rivals: "You don't see a lot of dancing robots or backflipping robots from Agility. We stuck to our strategy, which [are] these manual, very repetitive jobs, that it's hard to find humans to do."

Digit is already doing real work. The bipedal robot is deployed with nine customers, including Schaeffler, GXO, Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada and Mercado Libre, moving heavy totes in warehouses — more than 100,000 of them at a single GXO logistics facility. Agility was spun out of Oregon State University in 2015, years before the current physical-AI investment boom.

Why it matters

This is the first time investors can buy shares in a company that only builds humanoid robots. Figure AI, Tesla and Boston Dynamics are all private or part of much larger companies, so Agility's listing becomes the public market's purest bet on humanoid labour. The numbers show how early it still is: Agility earned just $174,000 in the first six months of 2026 — mostly from related parties — while losing $362 million in the same period. The clock is ticking on deploying the new capital well.

The bull case rests on Digit 5, the next-generation robot. Agility says it has $300 million in planned contracts from 30 customers for Digit 5, which can safely operate around humans rather than only inside an isolated work cell. Johnson said that pipeline was "one of the driving factors" behind the decision to go public. Digit 5 prototypes are due to be demonstrated at CES 2027 and widely available at the end of that year — though the deals depend on Digit 5 hitting undisclosed targets for throughput, uptime and placement accuracy.

What it means for you

For warehouse and logistics operators, the pitch is a robot that can lift a 50-pound object over and over without injury risk — a job Johnson says "you want to give that to the robot." Agility claims a robot can save a company $100,000 a year under its robot-as-a-service model, and even more if the customer buys the robot outright. Prospective customers pay $500,000 for an on-site proof of concept before a full deployment. For everyone else, Agility's listing is a public scoreboard: if a humanoid robot company can turn real warehouse hours into a sustainable business, the whole physical-AI sector gets a valuation anchor — and if it can't, the market will say so in public.

The Nodrits take

Agility is the anti-hype humanoid company: no backflips, no viral demos, just totes moved in real facilities — and 65,000 hours of real operational data from real customers to train on. That grounded record is exactly why it could list first. The open question is whether Digit 5 can deliver the dexterity and reliability its $300 million pipeline demands.
