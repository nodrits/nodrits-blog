Anthropic's Q3 Numbers Are In: $17 Billion in Revenue — and a Return to Operating Loss Ahead of Its Blockbuster IPO

November 2, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Anthropic is making billions — and still losing money. The AI company behind Claude reported third-quarter revenue of $16.96 billion in an amended IPO filing on Monday, November 2, 2026, more than twelve times the $1.38 billion it made a year earlier. Yet the same filing shows a swing back to a $567 million operating loss, and a staggering $895.4 billion in purchase and lease commitments, almost all of it for computing power and infrastructure.

What happened

Anthropic filed Amendment No. 1 to its S-1 registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, November 2. The original filing, made on October 26, stopped at June 30 — this amendment is the first to include the three months ended September 30, 2026. Revenue for that quarter reached $16.96 billion, up from $11.56 billion in the second quarter and $1.38 billion a year earlier. For the first nine months of 2026, revenue totalled $33.24 billion. The net loss for the quarter was $23.29 billion, compared with $12.1 billion a year earlier and $66.3 billion in the second quarter; according to AFP, that loss stemmed mostly from non-cash accounting charges linked to the company's rising valuation. On an operating basis, Anthropic swung to a $567 million loss after a $974 million operating profit in the second quarter, while total operating expenses jumped 292% year over year to $17.5 billion. The filing still leaves the IPO price range and share count blank; Anthropic plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, with its debut expected later in November.

Why it matters

This filing is being watched as a crucial test of investor appetite for AI companies, in Reuters' words, because it puts hard numbers on the AI spending boom. The headline tension is compute cost: computing and infrastructure expenses reached $9.86 billion in the quarter, or 58% of revenue, up from 48% in the second quarter. Anthropic attributes the operating swing mainly to $5 billion of computing capacity bought under costlier short-term commitments, plus $2 billion in non-cash charges tied to its employee donation-matching programme. Price reductions and contractual discounts also cut about $600 million from third-quarter revenue. On an adjusted basis, Anthropic still reported a $2.5 billion operating profit, up from $2.2 billion in the second quarter — but the gap between the adjusted profit and the standard-accounting loss is exactly what investors will argue about before the listing.

What it means for you

The commitments figure is the one to watch: about $895.4 billion in purchase and lease agreements, substantially all for computing and infrastructure, with about $69.8 billion due within 12 months of September 30, according to the filing. That is more than a full quarter's revenue coming due in a single year, and it explains why even a company growing revenue twelvefold year over year is not yet comfortably profitable on standard accounting. For businesses buying AI tools, the message is plain: the companies building frontier models are paying extraordinary prices for compute, and some of that pressure shows up as price changes, usage limits and shifting product terms. Judge an AI vendor on its unit economics, not just its growth chart.

The Nodrits take

Anthropic's quarter is the AI boom in miniature: breathtaking growth, breathtaking bills, and a profit line that depends on which accounting lens you use. The IPO will tell us whether public markets price that story on revenue, on adjusted profit, or on the $895 billion of promises already signed.
