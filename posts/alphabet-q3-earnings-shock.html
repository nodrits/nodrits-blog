Alphabet Q3 2026: Google Cloud Doubles as AI Spending Burns Cash — and the Stock Fell

October 29, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Google's parent company just delivered a quarter that confused Wall Street: revenue jumped 25% to $127.6 billion and its cloud business more than doubled — yet the stock fell more than 6% in after-hours trading. The reason is a single number that caught analysts completely off guard.

What happened

Alphabet reported its third-quarter 2026 results on Wednesday, October 28, after the US market closed. Total revenue came in at $127.6 billion, up 25% from $102.3 billion a year earlier, with operating income of $40.58 billion and net income of $26.17 billion. Earnings per share of $2.12 came in below the roughly $3.02 analysts expected.

The standout was Google Cloud: revenue more than doubled to $30.71 billion, up 103% year over year and ahead of the $29.57 billion analysts had forecast. Alphabet is the world's third-largest cloud provider, behind Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure, and the results showed enterprises pouring money into AI infrastructure and multi-year cloud deals.

But the shock was cash. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion — a far bigger cash burn than the $36 million analysts had estimated, according to data compiled by LSEG. The gap comes from the enormous capital spending needed to build data centers and buy chips for AI, and it sent shares sliding in extended trading.

Why it matters

Alphabet's quarter captures the central tension of the AI boom: demand is exploding, but so is the bill. CEO Sundar Pichai pointed to the momentum, saying the company is now on a half-trillion-dollar revenue run rate and that AI investments are driving the strongest Search query growth in five years. He also said Google Cloud revenue grew 103%, "powered by huge demand for our AI infrastructure and AI solutions," and that the Gemini app has now reached one billion users.

"Our AI investments are driving overall Search query growth, including commercial queries. In fact, our year-over-year query growth rate is the highest it's been in the last five years." — Sundar Pichai, CEO, Alphabet

Reuters notes that Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta are projected to collectively spend well over $700 billion in 2026 expanding AI data-center capacity, chips and networking. Alphabet's own investments in custom Tensor Processing Units alongside third-party GPUs helped it keep up with demand even as industry-wide chip and power bottlenecks persist.

What it means for you

For businesses, the message is two-sided. On one side, the cloud and AI build-out is accelerating — Google's first-party model APIs now process nearly 32 billion tokens per minute, more than four times last year's volume, which means AI tools and infrastructure will keep getting faster and cheaper to build on. On the other side, even the richest tech company on earth is feeling the cost: if Alphabet can burn through $9 billion in a quarter chasing AI capacity, smaller companies need to plan their own AI budgets carefully and pick tools that prove their return.

The Nodrits take

A record quarter and a falling stock in the same evening tells you everything about where tech is right now: investors are no longer impressed by AI growth alone — they want to see it pay for itself. Alphabet's bet is that today's spending becomes tomorrow's dominance in cloud and AI. Is that a smart long-term bet, or a cash-burning gamble? Tell us what you think in the comments.
