Opentrons Raises $40M: AI Designs the Experiment, a Robot Runs It

October 30, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

AI can design the experiment. Now a robot runs it. Opentrons Labworks, the New York company behind open, AI-native laboratory robots, announced a $40 million Series E on October 29, 2026, to scale the robotics, software and data infrastructure that let AI systems design, execute and learn from experiments — closing the gap between what AI can imagine and what a lab can test.

What happened

The round was led by healthcare investor Avego, with additional investment from BroadOak Capital Partners; existing investors include SoftBank Vision Fund and Khosla Ventures. Opentrons will use the capital to scale its robotics, software and data infrastructure tools, invest in AI-driven experimental execution, extend its automation capabilities, and expand into new markets and geographies. The company did not disclose its valuation. Its flagship Opentrons Flex platform combines benchtop robots with AI systems that enable experiments to be designed, run and improved in a continuous loop: AI-generated experimental plans connect to physical execution, results are collected as structured, reproducible data, and that data informs the next experiment. Founded in 2014 and headquartered in Long Island City, New York, Opentrons has deployed more than 11,000 robots to laboratories in over 80 countries — including at every top-20 US research university, 90% of the top 20 global pharmaceutical companies and 14 of the 15 largest global biopharma companies, per the company.

Why it matters

The bottleneck Opentrons targets is real and easy to miss: scientists can now use frontier models to generate hypotheses and design experiments far faster than laboratories can test them. The physical lab — pipetting, sample preparation, plate handling — has become the constraint on discovery, and millions of scientists still spend much of their day on manual work a robot can do. CEO James Atwood, PhD, put it directly: “Giving that time back to discovery is the biggest opportunity in science. We made lab automation accessible for any lab, and our systems are built for AI to run experiments directly. That is why autonomous science is standardizing on Opentrons.” The claim to standardization is the company's, not an industry verdict — but the deployment footprint gives it weight: when the same open, API-driven platform sits in thousands of labs, it becomes the execution layer other AI science tools have to work with.

What it means for you

Automate the repeatable first. List the pipetting, sample preparation and protocol steps eating your team's week, standardize those protocols before adding AI, and keep humans on experimental design and interpretation — the judgment, not the liquid handling. And treat reproducible data as the real asset: an experiment a robot ran identically twice is worth more than a brilliant one-off nobody can repeat.
