Apple Posts Record $114.5B Quarter as New CEO's First Earnings Beat Wall Street

November 3, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Apple has just delivered the biggest September quarter in its history. The company reported fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12% from a year ago and above Wall Street's estimate of about $113.4 billion, with profit of $30.7 billion and earnings of $2.10 per share. It is the first earnings report under new CEO John Ternus, who took over in September — and despite the beat, Apple shares slipped roughly 1% in volatile after-hours trading.

What happened

Apple announced the results on Monday, November 2, after US markets closed, for its fiscal fourth quarter ended September 26, 2026. iPhone revenue reached $57.58 billion, above the $56.54 billion analysts expected and a September-quarter record, driven by early demand for the iPhone 18 Pro lineup. Those iPhone sales did not include the new foldable iPhone Duo, which sold out within hours online last month. Mac revenue came in at $10.79 billion, well above the $9.61 billion estimate, while Services hit an all-time high of $31.57 billion, essentially in line with expectations. Not everything beat: iPad sales of $6.23 billion and wearables, home and accessories sales of $8.30 billion both came in below estimates. Gross margin was 47.9%, above the 47.5% estimate, including a roughly one-percentage-point benefit from tariff refunds. For the full fiscal year, Apple reported all-time record revenue of $479 billion, with $28 billion in operating cash flow in the quarter and $28 billion returned to shareholders.

Why it matters

The quarter matters because it shows how Apple is reshaping its own rhythm. After nearly two decades of launching most new iPhones each autumn, Apple is now spacing releases out — Pro and Duo models this year, other iPhone models next year — in a bid to smooth a yearly sales cycle that has long boomed at the holidays and faded by late summer. The company also raised prices to fight a surge in memory-chip costs, increasing iPhone 17 models by $100 and lifting some MacBook prices, which helped offset the squeeze. And there is a wrinkle in the success story: Apple says it cannot yet meet demand for the Duo, after customer reports of cracked screens on the foldable. Ternus told Reuters that only “a very small percentage” of Duo units had been returned for display issues and that Apple is replacing them, adding: “Duo is constrained as of today. The ramp is going well, and we’re working hard to get it to as many customers as quickly as possible, but at this point, we’re not able to predict when supply and demand will be in balance.” Meanwhile, Apple's AI-infused Siri is now fielding 2.5 billion requests a day. “Now we have the Siri that we always wanted, which we really built from the ground up,” Ternus said.

What it means for you

For anyone building on Apple's ecosystem, three signals stand out. First, the iPhone remains the engine: a 17% year-on-year jump in iPhone revenue means the installed base is still upgrading, so iOS-first products still reach the biggest paying audience. Second, Services at an all-time high keeps the App Store, subscriptions and payments flywheel spinning — distribution inside Apple's world is only getting more valuable. Third, the Duo supply crunch is a reminder that foldables are still a constrained, early market: if you are planning accessories, apps or campaigns around foldable hardware, expect limited early availability rather than a mass-market launch.

The Nodrits take

Our take: this was a beat built on pricing power as much as on products — higher prices and a staggered launch calendar did as much work as the new hardware itself. The open question is whether Apple can keep that formula going when memory costs keep climbing and the Duo stays supply-constrained.
