Kimi K3.1 Pro: Moonshot's 2.8-Trillion-Parameter Open Model Sits One Point Behind Claude on Terminal-Bench

October 24, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

China's Moonshot AI has released Kimi K3.1 Pro — the first model in its new K3.1 series — and it is one point behind Claude Opus 5.5 on the Terminal-Bench 4.0 agentic coding benchmark. The model packs roughly 2.8 trillion total parameters, a 1-million-token context window, and — crucially — full open weights are promised by November 4, 2026. This is the biggest open-model story of the week, and it resets the race between closed labs and open weights.

What happened

Moonshot AI launched Kimi K3.1 Pro on October 23/24, 2026, the first release in the K3.1 series. Moonshot describes it not as a new base model but as a post-trained Kimi K3 with far more reinforcement learning across "hundreds of downstream domains." It is live now across the Kimi ecosystem: the Kimi.ai app, the Kimi Work desktop app, Kimi Code, and the Kimi API (with Zero Data Retention available). A companion variant, Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed, offers the same capabilities at 2–3x the speed. The story was first reported by outlets including unite.ai, Startup Fortune, skimwire, madrobot.blog and panews.

Why it matters

The headline numbers are striking. On Moonshot's own, unaudited benchmarks, Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4% and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra at 58.2% — a gap of less than one point. On BenchCAD it reaches 92.7%, and Moonshot claims state-of-the-art on OfficeQA Pro v2 at 64.4. Its internal hallucination stress test cut claim-level error rates from 23.4% (K3) to 8.3%. Independent validation backs the direction: Artificial Analysis gives it an Intelligence Index score of 55, ranking 4th overall — behind only Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, and Muse Wonder Full.

The open-weights promise is the real earthquake: full weights downloadable and runnable by anyone on November 4, at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens — a 33% price cut versus K3 (Highspeed at $4/$20).

Moonshot also says it deliberately stripped cyberattack-related training data ahead of the open release to limit offensive use — a notable safety move in a landscape where open releases are often released with minimal hardening.

Key details

Architecture: mixture-of-experts with ~2.8 trillion total parameters and 104 billion active per token; multimodal text plus image; 1-million-token context window. Pricing: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (33% cheaper than K3); the Highspeed variant at $4/$20. Open weights: promised by November 4, 2026 — free to download and run. Availability today: Kimi.ai app, Kimi Work desktop app, Kimi Code, and the Kimi API with Zero Data Retention. All benchmark figures above are Moonshot's own numbers and unaudited; the Artificial Analysis index is the only independent measurement cited.

What it means for you

For businesses, freelancers, and agencies: a frontier-class agentic model at a 33% lower API price — and soon free to run yourself — changes the math on AI coding assistants and automation workflows. A 1-million-token context means entire codebases, documentation sets, or design systems can sit in one conversation. For anyone building AI features into client work, K3.1 Pro is a candidate to benchmark against your current Claude or GPT stack this week. For teams with data-sensitivity concerns, Zero Data Retention on the API plus full open weights in November removes two of the biggest objections to adopting third-party models.

The Nodrits take

The moat is no longer the weights — it is execution, distribution, and trust. Every open release of this caliber compresses the pricing power of closed labs and hands real leverage to small teams and agencies that can deploy fast. The question is no longer "which lab's model is best" but "who integrates it into products first."
