Hackers drained about €100 million from a French bank — and the money is already gone

November 5, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

French bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa said on Thursday, November 5, that about €100 million (around $112.6 million) is missing after a cyberattack this week — a rare successful heist against a large financial institution, and the stolen money may already have been converted into cryptocurrency. The bank says it cannot yet say how much it will be able to recover.

What happened

The attack hit on Wednesday, November 4. According to the bank, the attackers used an account belonging to Crédit Mutuel Arkéa itself to carry out fraudulent transactions to other banks, possibly allowing the funds to be converted into cryptocurrency through specialised platforms. The bank said a portion of the funds had been blocked and recovery efforts were underway. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT had flagged suspicious withdrawals earlier in the week, before the bank publicly confirmed the attack.

The bank says the attackers got in through a third-party service provider's platform — not through its online banking subsidiary Fortuneo, which the bank explicitly ruled out as the entry point. Customer accounts were not involved and customer assets were not affected, although the bank temporarily suspended some transactions as a precaution, delaying some transfers. The bank informed the European Central Bank, French banking supervisors and prosecutors, and the Paris prosecutor's office said its cybercrime unit had opened an investigation into an organised attack on an automated data processing system.

The exact final figure is still being evaluated: the bank has confirmed the scale of the losses but has not confirmed a precise amount. The bank also said the loss was not such as to undermine the financial health and solidity of the group — a notable statement given the lender earned a net profit of €230 million in the first half of the year.

Why it matters

Successful hacks of large financial institutions are rare — banks are among the most heavily defended targets in the world. This one worked because the attackers didn't need to break into customer accounts at all: they moved the bank's own money through its own account, via a third-party platform. That is a supply-chain weakness, not a password problem.

"A rare successful heist against a large financial institution." — Reuters, November 5, 2026

It also comes in a grim week for financial cybercrime: the Arkéa attack follows the theft last week of $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency from the Binance exchange, and a wave of cyberattacks targeting internet-connected devices in Europe. Regulators have been stepping up warnings about cyberattack risk, particularly as AI tools make large-scale fraud easier to run.

What it means for you

If you run a business, the lesson is not "buy more antivirus" — it is "audit your third parties." The entry point here was a service provider's platform, and the money moved through legitimate-looking transactions. Payment approvals, transaction limits, anomaly detection on outbound transfers, and strict controls on who can move money through partner platforms are the defences that matter. And if your business holds crypto or uses crypto exchanges, remember the trail here was spotted on-chain before the victim even confirmed the attack — blockchain transparency cuts both ways.

The Nodrits take

A €100 million bank heist with no masks, no guns and no customers harmed — just a compromised platform and fraudulent transfers. The scariest part is how ordinary the mechanism sounds. The question now is how much of the money can actually be clawed back once it has been converted to crypto.
