Google's Gemini 4 Carbon: The AI Model Built to Hunt Hackers

October 29, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Google has unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon, its newest and strongest frontier AI model — and it is built for one job above all: cybersecurity defense. Announced on October 28, 2026, just two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 with a score of 59, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 (58) and GPT-6 Astra (57). But there is a catch: almost nobody can use it yet.

What happened

Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, 2026, calling it "our strongest model with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities," in the words of DeepMind senior vice president Koray Kavukcuoglu. The model is designed for complex, long-running engineering and coding work, including multi-step bug root-cause analysis and patching across large codebases. On cybersecurity benchmarks, Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities, 54.8% on ExploitGym for exploitation validation, and 71.0% on CWE-bench for patching. Internal testing showed more than twice the success rate of Gemini 4 Argon at generating secure code, and Google says its Chrome team found Carbon produced high-quality patches more than 10 times better than another lab's large frontier model. Introductory pricing matches Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached inputs at 95% off, rising later to $4 and $20.

Why it matters

Carbon lands in the middle of an AI arms race where cyber-defense capability is the new frontier — the same day, Anthropic's Frontier Red Team reported an unreleased Claude chaining flaws into working browser and OS exploits. Google is framing Carbon as a defender's tool first: it is being made available this week only to a subset of cybersecurity defenders, then gradually to additional trusted partners through Google's Fairwind Program, while the company gathers feedback and iterates on guardrails.

"Today, we're announcing the next frontier model: Gemini 4 Carbon, our strongest model with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities." — Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP, Google DeepMind
Independent evaluator Artificial Analysis independently places Carbon first on its Intelligence Index, giving the launch credibility beyond Google's own benchmark claims — though VentureBeat notes tradeoffs, including a higher hallucination rate than Argon on one factual-knowledge test.

What it means for you

For most developers and businesses, Carbon is a wait-and-see story for now: broader access for developers, businesses and consumers comes only "as soon as possible" after guardrail work, with no date given. But the direction is clear — AI models are moving from writing code to finding and fixing security flaws in it, and the first access is going to the defenders. If your business ships software, expect AI-assisted vulnerability hunting and patching to become a standard part of the security toolkit within the year, and watch for Carbon's wider release and its $4/$20 standard pricing.

The Nodrits take

Google shipping its strongest model as a gated, defenders-first tool — rather than a wide consumer launch — says a lot about how seriously the labs now take dual-use risk. The benchmarks are impressive, but the real test will be what vetted defenders actually find with it in the wild.
