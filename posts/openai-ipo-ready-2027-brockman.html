OpenAI's President Was Asked About an IPO — His Two-Word Answer Says It All

November 6, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

OpenAI's president was asked about an IPO — and his two-word answer says it all. Greg Brockman told a Wall Street Journal tech conference on November 5, 2026 that the ChatGPT maker could be ready to go public in 2027. No date, no exchange, no share price — but the clearest public signal yet about the listing timetable.

What happened

Brockman, OpenAI's co-founder and president, was speaking on Wednesday at the Wall Street Journal tech conference in Napa, California, in a session titled “OpenAI's Race to Scale.” Asked on stage whether OpenAI would be ready for an initial public offering next year, he replied: “I think so.” He added that business customers now generate the majority of OpenAI's revenue — a major shift for a company once seen mainly as a consumer brand built around ChatGPT — and that annualised revenue grew 70% during the third quarter, with the enterprise business doubling. Brockman was candid about the first half of 2026, saying it had been difficult as rival Anthropic gained ground in enterprise AI and coding tools. OpenAI has already taken an initial step toward the public market: it confidentially submitted a draft registration statement to the Securities and Exchange Commission in June 2026, according to the Associated Press. Reports put annualised revenue at around $50 billion at the end of September, targeting more than $70 billion by year-end — reported estimates, not confirmed results, since the company has not published audited financials. OpenAI is also seeking fresh capital: it has secured around $11 billion in commitments for its latest round, below an original $30 billion target, with negotiations pointing to a valuation around $1.25 trillion.

Why it matters

The signal matters because of the race it sits inside. Anthropic is preparing for a potential public listing before the end of 2026, and Brockman's remarks are the first time OpenAI's leadership has publicly put a readiness window on its own debut. The enterprise shift is the substance behind the signal: a company that once lived on consumer subscriptions now earns most of its money from businesses, and Brockman framed that as the reason for his confidence — “I feel extremely good about the business. I feel like we've made so much progress this year, and I've honestly never felt more confident in what we're doing.” The funding round is the other half of the story: raising at a $1.25 trillion valuation while cutting the target from $30 billion to about $20 billion suggests a company balancing ambition against market appetite. And the caution is real: Brockman's answer was about readiness, not a promise, and nothing in his remarks sets a listing date, an exchange or an offering price.

What it means for you

If you build on OpenAI's platform, the practical takeaway is not the IPO — it is the enterprise pivot. A vendor whose revenue now comes mostly from businesses will prioritise business features, business pricing and business reliability, and the Codex agent push Brockman highlighted is aimed squarely at that market. If you are a founder or freelancer choosing AI tooling, watch what the public-market pressure does to pricing: public companies report quarterly, and the enterprise features that win deals are rarely the ones that stay cheapest. If you are simply watching the AI race, the number to remember is the revenue mix, not the valuation — a majority-enterprise OpenAI competes with Anthropic on contracts, not just on benchmarks.

The Nodrits take

Two AI labs are now racing to the stock market, and the winner may not be the one with the best model — it may be the one with the best enterprise book. OpenAI's answer to Anthropic's lead in business sales is a 70% quarterly revenue jump and a public readiness signal. The IPO itself is still a “when we feel is the right time” decision. But the direction is set: the chatbot era is ending, and the enterprise era — with its contracts, its quarterly reports and its public scrutiny — is beginning. Would you buy OpenAI stock on day one?
