OpenAI Closes $11 Billion in New Funding at a $1.25 Trillion Valuation — Well Short of Its $30 Billion Goal

November 7, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

OpenAI has closed on $11 billion in new funding — the first part of a round that values the ChatGPT maker at $1.25 trillion, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, November 6, according to Reuters. The money was wired to the company the same day. But the number tells a second story: OpenAI went into the fall seeking $30 billion or more at a roughly $1.4 trillion valuation, and this first close lands at barely a third of that target.

What happened

According to Bloomberg's reporting, carried by Reuters and citing people familiar with the matter, the $11 billion is only the initial tranche of the round. The full round could still grow to as much as $20 billion in the coming weeks as remaining commitments are completed. Investors from the United Arab Emirates are reported to be leading the round alongside Coatue. OpenAI has not publicly confirmed the figures.

This is a genuinely new development in a funding saga Nodrits has followed for weeks. On October 23 we covered OpenAI working on a $30 billion raise at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Then, on October 30, the Financial Times reported the target had been cut by $10 billion to about $20 billion after some investors reduced their allocations ahead of a Friday commitment deadline — and the Wall Street Journal reported roughly $11 billion had been committed so far. The difference now: the money is no longer a commitment on paper. It has actually closed and been wired.

For scale, OpenAI's last closed round was a $122 billion raise in March 2026 that valued the company at $852 billion. Even at the reduced $1.25 trillion figure, this round lifts that valuation by nearly half — but it also shows the company had to accept a lower price and a smaller raise than it first asked for.

Why it matters

The close lands in the middle of a brutal week for AI stocks. The Nasdaq fell sharply on November 5 as investors reassessed the AI infrastructure boom after Democrats swept the US midterm elections on a platform of tighter scrutiny for data centers — wiping trillions off tech valuations. OpenAI's chief financial officer, Sarah Friar, told CNBC on Friday that the company will press ahead with its US data-center expansion despite the political headwinds. The $11 billion gives OpenAI fresh cash to do exactly that, but the scaled-back round is a signal the market is now pricing AI's biggest name with real skepticism.

It also sharpens the race with Anthropic. Reuters has reported that prospective investors are questioning whether Anthropic can begin its IPO roadshow next week and stay on track for a November listing. Both labs are tapping a similar investor base, so OpenAI's first close — smaller than planned, at a lower valuation — sets a tougher reference price for everything that follows.

What it means for you

If you build on AI, don't read this as "AI is over." Read it as "AI money is getting pickier." Expect model providers to push harder on enterprise revenue, pricing discipline and proof of return — which is good news if you buy AI tools, because vendors will have to earn renewals. If you run a startup, the lesson is blunter: raise while you can show revenue, not just a roadmap, because the era of funding on vision alone is cooling fast.

The Nodrits take

The AI boom isn't ending — it's repricing. Eleven billion dollars is still an enormous raise by any normal standard; the story is that even OpenAI now has to negotiate. The winners from here won't be the labs that raise the most, but the ones that turn compute into cash flow.
