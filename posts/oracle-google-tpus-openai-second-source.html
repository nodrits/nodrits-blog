Oracle Will Rent Out Google's TPUs — and OpenAI Reportedly Wants to Buy Them

October 29, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Nvidia's biggest customers just found a second source. Oracle and Google announced on October 28, 2026 that Google's Tensor Processing Units will be offered on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure starting in 2027 — the first time a hyperscale cloud outside Google will rent them out — and, separately, OpenAI reportedly plans to buy Google's TPUs.

What happened

At its AI World conference in Las Vegas, Oracle said customers will be able to choose Google's Tensor Processing Units inside OCI, with Oracle supplying the power, cooling and networking. The joint announcement names Google's eighth-generation chips: TPU 8t for training, which Google says delivers nearly 3x the compute per pod of the previous generation, and TPU 8i for inference and agent workloads. Availability starts in calendar 2027 and ramps over several years. Oracle would not say how big the first deployment is, but OCI senior vice president Karan Batta told Data Center Dynamics it is a “multi-year, multi-gigawatt partnership with Google,” adding: “This is not an experiment.” The same day, Oracle and OpenAI expanded their partnership to bring Oracle's business apps into ChatGPT and power new Oracle products with OpenAI models and Codex. Separately, The Information reported, citing a person familiar with the matter, that OpenAI plans to buy Google's TPUs. Terms were not disclosed, and OpenAI declined to comment to Reuters while Google did not immediately respond — so treat the purchase as a plan, not a signed deal.

Why it matters

For most of the AI boom, “AI compute” has meant Nvidia, and Google's chips were something you could only rent inside Google Cloud — until April 2026, when Google said it would sell TPU hardware directly to a small group of customers. Anthropic is already a big TPU customer, and its IPO filing lists about $417 billion in new compute commitments, showing the scale of what these labs are buying. If OpenAI joins that list and Oracle's cloud resells the chips, Google becomes a real second supplier, and AI labs gain leverage on price. The honest caveat, from Alphabet's own CFO via press coverage: only a small share of TPU sales revenue will be booked this year, with most coming in 2027 — do not expect a quick revenue jump.

What it means for you

Do not hard-code your stack to one vendor. Benchmark your workload on TPUs before 2027 availability, watch Alphabet's earnings commentary for outside TPU demand, and treat the OpenAI report as a signal of intent, not a contract — the story to track is whether a second supplier actually changes prices, not just headlines.

The Nodrits take

Leverage is the real product here. Nvidia is not being replaced overnight, but the moment its best customers can choose, the price talks change. Is Nvidia's grip on AI compute finally loosening, or is this just leverage theater? Tell us in the comments on today's post.
