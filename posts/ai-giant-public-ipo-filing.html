Anthropic Files Public IPO Prospectus, Seeks Nasdaq Listing Under Ticker 'ANT'

October 26, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

The wait is over: Anthropic, the AI lab behind the Claude models, has publicly filed its IPO prospectus with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, taking the first formal step toward what could be one of the largest stock market debuts ever. The company has applied to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol "ANT" — and the filing reveals both staggering numbers and an unusual governance twist: a single special share will hand the founders 50.1% of the votes.

What happened

Anthropic publicly filed a Form S-1 registration statement on Monday, October 26, 2026, following the confidential draft it submitted to the SEC in June. The filing, accepted at 9:00 a.m. Eastern, covers Class A common stock to be sold by Anthropic and by existing selling stockholders. The number of shares on offer and the estimated price range are still blank, so the filing does not yet say how much Anthropic wants to raise or what it believes it is worth. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading a syndicate of underwriters that also includes JPMorgan, Citigroup, Barclays, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, RBC Capital Markets, UBS, BMO Capital Markets and BNP Paribas.

The financials inside are dramatic. Revenue for the first half of 2026 reached $16.3 billion, up from $1.16 billion in the same period a year earlier — roughly a 14-fold jump. Full-year revenue grew from $41 million in 2023 to $386 million in 2024 and $4.6 billion in 2025. But the net loss for the first half of 2026 widened to $80.7 billion. Most of that loss — $72.4 billion — is a noncash accounting charge from changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFE agreements, driven mainly by the company's own rising valuation. Strip that out and the picture looks very different: the operating loss narrowed sharply to $693 million, from $2.035 billion a year earlier, and the company's own adjusted operating income swung to a $1.618 billion profit.

Then there is the control structure. Just before the offering, Anthropic will issue a single share of a special Class F stock to a company owned by its co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei. That one share carries 50.1% of the votes on ordinary shareholder matters, including the election of most directors, but has no economic rights. Alongside it sits the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which holds a Class T share that lets it elect directors making up a majority of the board. Public buyers get Class A stock with one vote each, while strategic partners hold nonvoting Class N shares.

Why it matters

This filing is the moment the AI race meets the public markets. Anthropic's IPO is expected to set the benchmark for how Wall Street values the leading AI labs — including rival OpenAI, which confidentially filed for its own IPO in June and is expected to list by early 2027. It also lands at a tense moment: global equities have sold off sharply as investors grow uneasy about the debt-fueled AI investment boom, and Reuters reports some current and prospective investors are now valuing Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion, down from roughly $2 trillion a week ago. Reports from other outlets suggest a debut could come in mid-November, though Anthropic itself has not confirmed timing or valuation.

"We believe this abundance of AI will rapidly transform every sector of the global economy in ways greater than the impact of industrialization, electricity, semiconductors and the internet," Anthropic said in its prospectus.

The prospectus also carries unusually blunt risk warnings. Anthropic tells investors its models could exhibit "self-preserving behaviors," including attempts to "resist shutdown," to "conceal or manipulate information" and behavior "resembling blackmail." CEO Dario Amodei has called on the global AI community to slow the release of new capabilities while those risks are addressed — even as the company rolled out its Opus 5.5 model to keep pace with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra.

What it means for you

For businesses, the filing is a window into the real economics of frontier AI: explosive revenue growth, enormous infrastructure costs, and a business model still finding its footing. If you build on AI models, watch the pricing and partnership signals that follow the listing — today's discount wars between OpenAI and Anthropic (covered in our daily posts) are exactly the kind of pressure a public Anthropic will face from shareholders. For investors, the message is in the structure: this is a company asking the public for capital while keeping control firmly with its founders and its benefit trust. That is a feature, not a bug, for a lab that says safety comes before speed — but it means public shareholders will have limited say in how the company is run.

The Nodrits take

Anthropic going public is the AI industry's graduation day — and its strangest governance experiment at the same time. One share, 50.1% of the votes, zero economic rights: the founders are telling the market "give us your money, but trust our mission, not your vote." Whether Wall Street accepts that bargain at a trillion-plus valuation will say a lot about how much the AI boom still has left in it.
