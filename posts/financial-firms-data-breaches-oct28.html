Three South Korean Financial Firms Report Data Breaches in a Single Day

October 28, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Three South Korean financial firms reported data breaches on Wednesday, October 28: crypto exchange Bithumb, brokerage Daishin Securities and asset manager Mirae Asset Global Investments. Daishin alone confirmed personal information belonging to 38,490 customers had leaked, while Bithumb and Mirae Asset were still assessing the scope of their incidents when the reports landed.

What happened

Reuters, citing Yonhap News Agency, reported the three disclosures on Wednesday. Bithumb said its in-house mobile system had been hacked and personal information leaked, and it was investigating whether member data was included; Yonhap-linked reporting says the firm reported the incident to the Korea Internet & Security Agency and the Personal Information Protection Commission. Daishin Securities said personal information of 38,490 customers had leaked after it detected signs of unauthorised access on October 17 to a server supporting remote securities-account opening; it confirmed the leak on October 26, took emergency measures and notified customers and regulators. The exposed Daishin categories included names, email addresses, postal addresses, communications IDs, bank names, bank account numbers, securities account numbers and mobile phone numbers, with account numbers and mobile numbers encrypted, according to Aju Press. Mirae Asset Global Investments confirmed a hacking incident, with authorities assessing the situation, but had not disclosed the scope at the time of reporting. The reports do not say the three incidents are linked.

Why it matters

The disclosures land inside a wider wave: South Korea's Financial Services Commission said earlier this month that cyber incidents had been identified at 16 financial firms, with roughly 100,000 cases of data leaks so far. That context is what makes a single day with three more names matter — it suggests a sector-wide exposure problem rather than three isolated accidents, even though each firm's incident and response remain separate. Separately, Seoul's Science Ministry and the Korea Internet & Security Agency urged website operators to check for PHP flaws that let attackers run commands on a server without logging in, Reuters noted in the same report.

What it means for you

If you hold accounts with an affected firm, watch for its official notice rather than acting on rumours: check which data categories were involved, change reused passwords, turn on two-factor authentication, and treat unexpected messages asking for account details with extra suspicion in the days after a breach. For any business handling customer data, the pattern is the same lesson every time — detection, containment and a clear customer notice matter as much as prevention, and encrypted fields are what keep a leak from becoming a takeover.

The firms that confirmed numbers and categories quickly give customers something to act on; the ones still assessing scope leave them guessing.
