NVIDIA Puts $3 Billion Behind Meta's Muse in Expanded AI Alliance

October 27, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

NVIDIA is putting $3 billion of its own money into Meta — and the reason is one AI assistant. On Tuesday, October 27, 2026, the two companies announced an expanded partnership built around Meta's Muse assistant and the Muse family of AI models, with NVIDIA investing $3 billion in Meta's equity offering as part of the deal.

What happened

NVIDIA and Meta said they will partner to expand their work on Muse and the Muse models to improve inference and training performance, citing popular demand for useful AI agents. According to NVIDIA's announcement, Meta's Muse models and agents — which run on NVIDIA infrastructure — have delivered frontier-level benchmark performance and surging consumer downloads since launch. The companies plan to deepen their collaboration with optimizations across workflows to maximize performance and improve total cost of ownership.

The deal also deepens the hardware side of the relationship. Meta intends to expand its deployment of NVIDIA infrastructure, including Vera Rubin systems and Vera CPUs for next-generation AI agents, plus NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet networking and BlueField-4 to support network efficiency and throughput. The companies are also considering further collaboration on NVIDIA's OpenShell Reference Architecture — including OpenShell for safe agent deployment and NVIDIA Sentry for monitoring agent behavior.

Why it matters

This is one of the largest single endorsements of an AI assistant yet: a chipmaker investing billions directly in the company building the assistant, rather than simply selling it hardware. The investment lands inside Meta's wider equity raise announced on October 26 to fund its AI infrastructure buildout, and it ties NVIDIA's fortunes even more closely to Muse's success.

"AI agents represent a new computing paradigm — software that understands our goals, reasons, uses tools and gets work done on our behalf. NVIDIA and Meta are expanding our partnership to optimize the computing infrastructure end to end for capable, efficient and secure AI agents, so Muse can empower billions of people to achieve more and pursue greater ambitions." — Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of NVIDIA

The agent angle is the strategic core. Both companies are betting that AI's next phase is not chatbots that answer questions, but agents that act — and that the winners will be whoever controls the full stack, from the silicon to the assistant. NVIDIA supplying the infrastructure, the safety tooling and now $3 billion of capital makes that bet explicit.

What it means for you

For businesses and builders, the signal is that AI agents are moving from experiment to infrastructure. When the world's most valuable chip company and one of the world's largest app platforms align this deeply around one assistant, expect agent capabilities — and the infrastructure to run them — to become a default layer of everyday software, not a premium add-on. If your product or workflow depends on AI tools, the agent wave is the one to plan for now.

The Nodrits take

The $3 billion figure is the headline, but the real story is the depth of the integration: chips, networking, safety tooling and capital, all pointed at one assistant. The AI race is consolidating around a handful of full-stack alliances — and Muse just got the strongest backing of any assistant so far.
