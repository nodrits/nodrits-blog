Perplexity Just Hired AI Coworkers — They Never Clock Out

November 7, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Perplexity just hired your next coworker — and it never clocks out. The company launched Teammates on November 5, 2026: always-on AI agents that live inside a shared workspace, own a piece of ongoing work around the clock, and hand tasks to each other while you do something else. The price is the catch: $200 a month.

What happened

Perplexity announced Teammates on November 5, 2026, with CEO Aravind Srinivas posting on X — “Teammates work for you” — as reported by The Verge and others the same day. Teammates are personal agents for work, built on Perplexity Computer, the company's existing agent platform. Each teammate owns a responsibility rather than answering a single request: it has its own thread, its own desktop and browser, and memory between conversations. Teammates can work simultaneously, pass tasks to one another, and collaborate through Slack. New teammates start on Claude Opus 5.5 by default, and you can pick a different model per teammate or per task. The launch is a research preview for individual Max subscribers on the web — Max costs $200 a month, or $167 a month billed annually — with native mobile and desktop apps coming soon. Enterprise Pro and Enterprise Max customers are not included by default, and audit logs are gated at 50 seats. Srinivas says Perplexity's own workspace has used about 10,000 teammates in the last 30 days, and the company says its internal Slack integration produced $1.6 million of work for 300 employees in four weeks — a company claim with no published definition of how that work was valued.

Why it matters

Teammates completes a three-way race that started this season: OpenAI launched Dots on September 29 at DevDay, Anthropic opened Claude Duet as a research preview, and now Perplexity has its own always-on agent. The shift is the point: the industry is moving from prompts to goals — you assign a responsibility, and the agent keeps working until it is done, instead of waiting for your next message. That is also why the price matters. At $200 a month, Teammates is a premium feature aimed at users willing to pay for ongoing assistance, and the launch announcement does not spell out whether the price is per user or for a team, how much supervision the agents need, or what the credit burn looks like on a real workload — the questions that decide whether a $200 coworker is a bargain or a burn. The honest framing: this is a research preview, not a finished product, and the first reviews will be written by the Max subscribers who try it on real work.

What it means for you

If you already pay for Perplexity Max, the question is what ongoing work you would hand over first. Start with work you can check — research, monitoring, drafting, triage — not decisions you cannot undo, and watch the credit meter: Teammates draws on Max's 10,000 monthly Computer credits, so a busy teammate can burn a month's allowance fast. If you are on Free or Pro, the decision is a $200-a-month one, and the honest test is a single month of real work, not the launch demo. If you run a team, note the limits before planning around Teammates: Enterprise is not included by default, audit logs need 50 seats, and there is no published per-task credit rate. And if you build agents, note the design: each teammate gets its own computer, its own memory and its own Slack presence — the agent is becoming a colleague with a desk, not a tool with a prompt box.

The Nodrits take

A $200-a-month coworker that never sleeps is either a bargain or a burn — and the meter, not the marketing, will decide which. The always-on era is now a three-way race, and its real question is not whether agents can work while you sleep. It is what happens to the definition of a job when they do. Teammates is the most expensive answer so far, and the most direct: you do not prompt it, you employ it. Would you hire an AI coworker for $200 a month? Tell us in the comments on today's post.
