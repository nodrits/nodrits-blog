Infisical Raises $40M: The Vault That Keeps AI Agents Away From Your Passwords

October 28, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

Your AI agent needs the keys to everything — your databases, your code, your cloud. It should never actually hold them. San Francisco startup Infisical has raised $40 million at a $600 million valuation to make sure of it, with Agent Vault: a way to give agents access to company systems without ever giving them the passwords and API keys themselves.

What happened

Infisical announced a $40 million Series B on October 27, 2026, at a $600 million valuation, led by investor Elad Gil — who also led the company's $16 million Series A in June 2025 — with Gradient and Y Combinator among the other investors. Infisical provides cybersecurity infrastructure for securing sensitive credentials, managing access to critical systems and keeping digital certificates renewed: its open-source project has more than 30,000 GitHub stars, and its customers include Airbus, Volkswagen, Hugging Face and Wise. The company says it serves more than 1,300 customers and 300,000 developers and secures 10 billion secrets daily. The new funding goes to product development, hiring across engineering and go-to-market, and expanding its agent security infrastructure. At the centre of the round is Agent Vault, which lets companies define exactly what each agent can access, so an agent can do its job without credentials that could leak in a cyberattack or be passed, accidentally, to a large language model provider or another tool.

Why it matters

Agents are a new class of enterprise user, and they break the old security model: a human employee can be trained not to paste a password into a chat window, but an agent's entire job is to pass data between systems. A key sitting in an agent's context, log or tool call is a breach waiting for a reader. Investors have noticed: identity startup Oak emerged with $60 million and NewCore raised $66 million to manage agent credentials, part of a wave that pushed cybersecurity funding to $6.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, according to Dealroom. Infisical's bet is that secrets management — boring, essential, and already embedded in developer workflows — becomes the control plane for the agent era. As co-founder and CEO Vlad Matsiiako put it: “You wouldn't give an employee unrestricted access to every system in your company, and AI agents shouldn't be any different.”

What it means for you

Treat agents like employees, not tools. Give each agent least-privilege access — only the systems its job needs — scope and rotate keys per agent and per job, log every system an agent touches, and never paste a real key into a prompt or an agent chat. If your agent platform cannot show you exactly what an agent can reach, that is the gap to close before you widen its permissions.

The Nodrits take

The vault is the new firewall. Matsiiako's line is the whole thesis: agents should not be any different from employees. The companies that learn that first will leak last. Should AI agents ever hold real passwords and API keys? Tell us in the comments on today's post.
