Upwind Raises $400M Series C at $3.8B Valuation to Secure Cloud and AI Infrastructure

October 28, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 4 min read

Upwind Security has raised $400 million in a Series C round led by Bessemer Venture Partners, valuing the cloud and AI security startup at $3.8 billion. The round, announced on October 28, 2026, more than doubles the $1.5 billion valuation from its $250 million Series B in January 2026 — and brings its total funding to over $800 million since it was founded in 2022.

What happened

Upwind, a San Francisco-based startup founded in 2022 by Amiram Shachar, Tal Zur and Lavi Ferdman, announced the $400 million Series C on its official blog on October 28, 2026, and Bloomberg reported the same figures the same day. Bessemer Venture Partners led the round, with participation from existing investors Greylock Partners, Cyberstarts, Craft Ventures, Salesforce Ventures, Alta Park, Picture Capital, Swish Ventures, Cerca Partners, TCV, Penny Jar and Leaders Fund, plus new investor Canapi Ventures. Bloomberg puts Upwind’s total raised since founding at $830 million; Upwind itself says total funding now exceeds $800 million. Its previous round, a $250 million Series B in January 2026, was also led by Bessemer and valued the company at $1.5 billion.

Why it matters

The round lands as companies rush AI agents and applications into the cloud — and attackers follow them there. Upwind’s platform monitors cloud applications and AI agents in real time, detecting threats such as malware or suspicious activity in cloud infrastructure and responding automatically, rather than relying only on periodic scans. As Upwind put it in its announcement, the new capital will fuel product and engineering growth “in building the platform that will secure cloud & AI infrastructure for the world in the AI era.” A valuation jumping from $1.5 billion to $3.8 billion in under nine months signals how much investors are willing to pay for security built for the AI-agent era.

What it means for you

If your business runs anything in the cloud — a website, a SaaS app, an AI chatbot or an automated workflow — the attack surface now includes the AI tools themselves, not just your servers. Runtime monitoring, which watches what is actually happening inside live workloads, is becoming the baseline rather than a luxury. For small and mid-sized businesses, the practical lesson is to ask any cloud or AI vendor how live threats are detected and answered, not just how systems are configured at setup.

Upwind’s rise shows security is no longer an afterthought bolted onto AI adoption — it is where some of the biggest cheques in tech are now going.
