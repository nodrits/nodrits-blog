Collective Lands $100M: The AI Finance Department for the One-Person Business

October 30, 2026 · Nodrits Solutions · 3 min read

The one-person business just got a finance department. Collective, a San Francisco startup building an AI-native financial operating system for solo entrepreneurs, announced $100 million in new funding on October 29, 2026 — and its pitch is simple: a single person should be able to run a business with the financial muscle of a much larger company, without hiring the team.

What happened

The package combines a $40 million Series B equity round led by General Catalyst, QED Investors and Gradient Ventures, with Intuit Ventures, FT Partners and SemperVirens participating, and a $60 million growth facility from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund, designated to fuel customer acquisition. The Customer Value Fund is not ordinary equity: it pays for customer acquisition and is repaid from the revenue those customers generate. Collective says the new capital will also accelerate product development in banking and insurance. The platform covers the full back office for what it calls Businesses-of-One: company formation (including LLCs), accounting that categorizes expenses and reconciles accounts, invoicing, payroll, autonomous tax preparation and filing, business and health insurance, and customer support. AI automates much of the background work — the company says it answers about half of all support requests without a human — while people verify work and handle approvals and complex queries.

Why it matters

The timing tracks a real shift in who runs businesses. Collective cites roughly 30 million solo businesses in the United States, and Stripe research it points to showing the number of solo operators generating $1 million a year doubled between 2023 and 2025, while those reaching $10 million tripled. As one person's output grows, the back office becomes the bottleneck: disconnected tools for bookkeeping, payroll and taxes, plus high fees to traditional accountants, eat the hours that should go to growth. Co-founder and CEO Hooman Radfar's framing is blunt: “Traditional accounting firms scale by adding people. Collective scales with intelligence.” The company reports triple-digit year-over-year growth and says members have saved a combined $350 million in taxes — company figures, not audited financials, but they explain why investors are betting on the category. Monthly bookkeeping capacity, Radfar told PYMNTS, rose from 40 closes to 300 by summer, with a target of 600; a traditional bookkeeper, he said, handles about 40.

What it means for you

If you freelance or run a one-person business, systematize before you scale. Separate business and personal money first, automate invoicing and expense capture so nothing is reconstructed at tax time, and review your tax setup before revenue jumps — that is when mistakes get expensive. The transferable lesson from Collective is not the product but the order of operations: buy back hours, not just software, and keep a human checking the numbers that matter.
